**Rating Protocol:**

**Step 1: Initial Scan for Deal-Breakers (Automatic 1-Star)**
First, analyze the review for any "deal-breakers"—critical issues that make the product or experience fundamentally unacceptable. If the review meets any of the criteria below, you must assign a **1-Star** rating and stop the evaluation.

*   **Critical Failure or Misrepresentation:** The product is non-operational, broken, missing key components, or was so frustrating that the user abandoned it. The experience completely fails to meet its basic promise, leading to severe disappointment.
    *   *Examples: "it was dead on arrival," "the main piece was missing," "I couldn't get it to work and gave up," "I stopped reading halfway through because it was awful."*

*   **Harmful Outcome:** The product caused damage to the user, their data, or other property.
    *   *Examples: "it bricked my phone," "caused a severe skin reaction."*

**Step 2: Deconstruct the Review into Pros and Cons**
If no deal-breaker is identified, proceed to break the review down. Create a simple list of all positive points (Pros) and negative points (Cons) mentioned by the user. This breakdown is essential for the next step.

*   **Pros:** List the specific features or aspects the user liked (e.g., "fast performance," "beautiful cover art," "comfortable fit").
*   **Cons:** List the specific flaws, drawbacks, or complaints (e.g., "poor battery life," "the plot twist was predictable," "the material feels cheap").

**Step 3: Apply the Cascading Scoring Rubric**
Evaluate the review against the following rules in the exact order presented. The final star rating is determined by the **first rule that applies**.

*   **Rule for 5 Stars (Outstanding / Enthusiastic Endorsement):**
    *   **Condition:** The user is overwhelmingly happy, and the product met or surpassed all expectations. The praise is specific and effusive. A 5-star review can acknowledge a minor downside but must frame it as insignificant in the face of overall excellence.
    *   **Identify:** Overwhelmingly positive language and a tone of delight. Look for words like "love," "perfect," "best," "flawless," "amazing," or a detailed account of why the product is an ideal fit for them. The user is thrilled.
    *   **Example:** "The battery life is just okay, but the screen is so gorgeous and the speed is so fast that I don't even care. This is the best tablet I've ever owned."

*   **Rule for 4 Stars (Very Good / Solid Endorsement with Caveats):**
    *   **Condition:** The user is clearly satisfied and would recommend the product, but they have a specific, noteworthy reservation that tempers their enthusiasm. The overall sentiment is strongly positive.
    *   **Identify:** A "great, but..." pattern. The review offers strong, specific praise alongside a non-trivial critique. The language is positive ("great," "excellent," "really good") but lacks the "love/best/perfect" superlatives of a 5-star review. Generic praise like "it was a good book" is not sufficient for 4 stars.
    *   **Example:** "The camera on this phone is absolutely incredible! The photos are professional quality. However, the battery barely lasts a full day, which is a definite drawback."

*   **Rule for 3 Stars (Average / Mixed / "It's Fine"):**
    *   **Condition:** This is the neutral rating. The user's experience was mixed, the product was merely adequate, or it was a letdown that wasn't a complete failure.
    *   **Identify one of these patterns:**
        *   **A) Balanced Opinion:** The review contains a roughly equal number of significant Pros and Cons, with neither side clearly outweighing the other.
        *   **B) Explicitly Average:** The user describes the product as functional but unremarkable. This is the correct rating for simple, non-enthusiastic statements like "It was okay," "Good," "Decent," "Fine," or "I liked it." A simple "Good book" falls here.
        *   **C) Mildly Disappointed:** The product works but didn't live up to the user's hopes. They might say "I was expecting more" or "not as good as the last version," even if they don't list many specific flaws.
    *   **Example:** "It's a decent vacuum for the price. It picks up pet hair well, but the cord is way too short and it's very loud. It gets the job done." or "The story was interesting and kept me engaged. Good book."

*   **Rule for 2 Stars (Predominantly Negative / Not Recommended):**
    *   **Condition:** The review has passed the "deal-breaker" test from Step 1 but is still dominated by significant flaws. The product is functional but a major disappointment. The user is clearly dissatisfied and would advise against buying it.
    *   **Identify:** The negative points are the main focus of the review. Any positive comments are minor or seem like faint praise ("the color was nice, but..."). The overall tone is one of warning or frustration. Look for keywords like "disappointing," "poorly made," "frustrating," "would not recommend."
    *   **Example:** "While the design is sleek, the software is incredibly buggy and crashes constantly. I managed to get it working, but the whole experience has been a headache. I regret this purchase."

*   **Rule for 1 Star (Deal-Breaker Confirmed):**
    *   **Condition:** A deal-breaker was found during the initial scan in Step 1.
    *   **Example:** "I was so excited for this, but it arrived with the screen completely shattered. Useless. Getting a refund."
