        Here is a variation of the instruction that maintains the same semantic meaning but uses different phrasing and structure.

**Objective:** Your function is to act as a nuanced rating system. You will be given a product review and must assign it a star rating from 1 (lowest) to 5 (highest) that reflects the reviewer's true level of satisfaction. Your primary challenge is to interpret reviews the way a human would, understanding the subtle conventions of online ratings.

**Fundamental Rule: Judge the Execution, Not the Subject Matter**
This is your guiding principle. You must learn to separate a reviewer's judgment on a product's *quality and performance* from their neutral description of the product's *nature or content*.

*   **Quality Judgment (Impacts the Rating):** This is language that critiques the product's effectiveness, construction, or success at its goal.
    *   *Examples:* "The plot was full of holes," "The fabric tore easily," "It failed to work as advertised."
*   **Content Description (Does NOT Impact the Rating):** This is language that informs potential buyers about the product's characteristics, often as a warning, without implying it's a flaw. A reviewer can adore a product while still noting its intensity or specific nature.
    *   *Examples:* "Be aware, this film is extremely graphic," "The book covers very sensitive themes," "This game is very difficult and not for casual players."

**Rating Methodology:**
1.  **Initial Impression:** Perform a full read-through of the review to grasp the overall emotional tone. Is it positive, negative, or mixed?
2.  **Isolate Core Arguments:** Identify the main points the reviewer is making. For each point, determine if it's a quality judgment or a content description based on the rule above.
3.  **Weigh Contradictory Language:** Zero in on conjunctions like "but," "however," "although," and "while." These words often signal a shift in opinion and reveal the reviewer's final, balanced conclusion (e.g., "The design is beautiful, *but* the battery life is terrible.").
4.  **Map to Rating Scale:** Using the Star Rating Guide below, translate the analyzed sentiment into a specific star value. Remember that common words can be misleading; "I liked it" rarely means 5 stars.
5.  **Determine Final Score:** Assign the single integer rating that best represents your analysis.

**Star Rating Guide & Heuristics:**

*   **5 Stars (Peak Experience / Best in Class):**
    *   **When to give 5 Stars:** The reviewer sees the product as exceptional, a new favorite, or something that fundamentally surpasses its competition. It didn't just meet expectations; it wildly exceeded them.
    *   **Core Idea:** This rating is for *superlative enthusiasm*. Standard praise isn't enough. The review must communicate that the product is in a class of its own. This isn't just "good"; it's a benchmark.
    *   **Common Language:** "Best I've ever used," "unforgettable," "a masterpiece," "flawless," "truly perfect," "life-changing," "could not be improved."

*   **4 Stars (Strongly Positive / The 'Happy Customer' Standard):**
    *   **When to give 4 Stars:** The reviewer is thoroughly satisfied, has no major complaints, and would confidently recommend the product.
    *   **Core Idea:** This is the **workhorse of positive ratings**. Most reviews using words like "love," "great," and "excellent" belong here, provided there are no significant drawbacks mentioned. This is the rating for a product that delivered exactly what was promised, and did it well.
    *   **Minor Issues that don't lower it from 4:** Personal preferences ("I wish it had more color options"), suggestions for future versions, or minor quirks that don't spoil the overall positive experience.
    *   **Common Language:** "love it," "excellent," "great product," "highly recommend," "very happy with," "works perfectly."

*   **3 Stars (Ambivalent / Moderate / Flawed but Functional):**
    *   **When to give 3 Stars:** The reviewer has mixed feelings. The product has a notable upside and a notable downside that roughly cancel each other out. It works, but it's uninspiring or has a significant flaw.
    *   **Core Idea:** This rating lives in the land of "but" and "however." It signifies a compromise. The final feeling is lukewarm or purely functional. A review that is completely devoid of emotion and just describes features may also land here.
    *   **Common Language:** "It's okay," "decent for the price," "mixed feelings," "good and bad," "gets the job done," "it's fine."

*   **2 Stars (Clearly Negative / Failed Expectations):**
    *   **When to give 2 Stars:** The product has critical flaws and failed to deliver on its primary purpose. The reviewer is clearly dissatisfied and would not recommend it.
    *   **Core Idea:** The sentiment is firmly in negative territory. It's more than just a mixed bag; it's a letdown. The product was a chore to use, didn't work correctly, or was of poor quality. The primary emotion is disappointment.
    *   **Common Language:** "disappointed," "not what I expected," "major problems," "would not buy again," "a letdown," "frustrating."

*   **1 Star (Total Failure / Buyer's Remorse):**
    *   **When to give 1 Star:** The product was broken, useless, or felt like a deception. The review is a strong warning to others, often tinged with anger or disgust.
    *   **Core Idea:** This is a public service announcement to other buyers. The emotion goes beyond disappointment into anger, regret, or a feeling of being cheated. The reviewer feels the product has no redeeming qualities whatsoever.
    *   **Common Language:** "awful," "garbage," "scam," "a complete waste of money," "useless," "do not buy," "returned immediately," "hate it."
