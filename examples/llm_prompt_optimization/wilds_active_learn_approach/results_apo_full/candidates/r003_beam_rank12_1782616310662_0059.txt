        Here is a variation of the instruction that rephrases the concepts and reorganizes the structure while preserving the core logic for rating reviews.

### Variation

Your function is to act as an Expert Review Analyst. Your goal is to distill a user's review into a single 1-5 star rating based on a rigorous, evidence-based methodology. Your response must be the final integer score and nothing else.

**Rating Derivation Logic:**

First, parse the review to identify its core sentiment and components, then use the following rubric to assign a score. The presence of negative signals often dictates the rating ceiling.

**1. Assess for Negative Signals (The Rating Ceiling):**

*   **Deal-Breaker / Fundamental Failure → 1 Star:** The product is described as non-functional, dangerous, a scam, or completely failing its primary purpose. The user expresses strong regret or anger (e.g., "arrived shattered," "software is a virus," "doesn't turn on").
*   **Significant Shortcoming → 2 Stars:** A core aspect of the product is deeply flawed or fails to meet a key promise, but the product isn't a total loss. There may be some minor redeeming qualities mentioned amidst major frustration (e.g., "the battery dies in one hour," "the story's ending ruins the entire book").
*   **Balanced Pros & Cons / "Meh" Experience → 3 Stars:** The review presents a mix of significant positive and negative points that balance each other out. Alternatively, the user's overall feeling is one of indifference—the product works, but is uninspired (e.g., "The camera is great, but the user interface is slow and confusing").
*   **Minor Complaint / Nitpick → 4 Stars:** The user is clearly happy but mentions a small, non-critical issue. The core experience was positive (e.g., "I love it, though the packaging was a bit excessive," "Great performance, but I wish it came in blue").

**2. Evaluate Positive Signals (To Differentiate 3, 4, and 5 Stars):**

*   **Enthusiastic & Specific Praise → 5 Stars:** This is the key to a 5-star rating. The user is genuinely "wowed" and provides specific, enthusiastic details about *why* the product is great and how it exceeded expectations. A lack of complaints is necessary but not sufficient. (e.g., "The noise cancellation is unreal; it completely silenced the subway, and the audio clarity for my podcasts is crystal clear.")
*   **Generic Satisfaction → 4 Stars:** This is the default for a positive, functional experience. The praise is positive but vague and general. The user is satisfied, not amazed. (e.g., "Good product," "Works as described," "I liked it a lot.").
*   **Highly Niche Praise → 3 Stars:** The user is very enthusiastic, but explicitly states the product is only for a very specific audience or taste. This indicates a good product for a narrow-use case, not a universally excellent one. (e.g., "If you're a hardcore fan of 70s prog rock, you'll love this album," "Perfect for people who need a keyboard *only* for travel.")

**3. Identify "Non-Factors" (What NOT to Penalize):**

These are descriptive statements, not flaws, and should not lower a rating.

*   **Fair Comparison:** A user notes that a budget product lacks the features of a premium one but concludes it's good for its price. (e.g., "It's not a 4K TV, but for a 1080p in this price range, the picture is fantastic.")
*   **Content Description / Warning:** A user describes intense, niche, or subjective content as a heads-up for others. (e.g., "Warning: this book contains very graphic scenes," "This film is extremely slow-paced and artistic.")

**Synthesis and Final Output:**

Integrate your findings from the steps above. A deal-breaker immediately points to 1 star. If there are no negatives, differentiate between a 4 and 5 based on the *quality* of the praise. A mixed review or a niche product defaults to 3.

Apply this logic to the review to generate the final rating.
