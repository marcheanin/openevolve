**Objective: Determine the Star Rating from a Product Review**

Your mission is to analyze the text of a product review and assign it a star rating from 1 to 5. The rating should accurately reflect the reviewer's final verdict on the product.

This process is governed by two hierarchical directives. You must follow them in order.

### **Primary Directive: Scan for Explicit Ratings**

This is the cardinal rule and supersedes any sentiment analysis.

Your first and most important action is to scan the entire review for a numerical rating provided by the user.
-   Examples include: "I give this 5 stars", "2/5", "a solid 9 out of 10", "one-star product".
-   If you find such a number, it is the conclusive rating. Normalize this rating to a 5-point scale (e.g., 9/10 becomes 4.5, which rounds to 5 stars; 1/10 becomes 0.5, which rounds to 1 star). This number is the final answer; cease all further sentiment analysis.

### **Secondary Protocol: Holistic Sentiment Analysis (When No Explicit Rating is Present)**

Only proceed to this step if the Primary Directive yields no explicit rating.

**Guiding Principle: Ascertain the Reviewer's Final Verdict**
Your task is to determine the ultimate message the reviewer wants to convey. Avoid a simplistic tally of positive/negative terms. Instead, synthesize the entire text to understand their concluding opinion. Ask yourself: "What is the single most important takeaway from this review? Is it a strong recommendation, a cautious one, a neutral shrug, or a clear warning?"

**Analytical Procedure:**

1.  **Establish Overall Tone:** Perform an initial read-through to get a general sense of the reviewer's emotion (e.g., ecstatic, pleased, indifferent, frustrated, furious).
2.  **Identify the "But" Factor:** Pinpoint the review's pivot point. Contrastive words like "but," "however," "though," and "that said" are critical. The sentiment expressed *after* these words typically carries more weight and often reveals the true conclusion.
3.  **Pinpoint Conditional Language:** This is a vital step for distinguishing neutral or mixed reviews. Actively search for praise or criticism that is dependent on a condition. These qualifiers are strong indicators that a review is not a simple 4 or 5-star endorsement.
    *   **Examples of conditional praise (often signals a 3-star rating):** "Good, **considering the price**," "A decent option **if you can't find anything else**," "It's a fun read **for die-hard fans**," "It's fine **as a starting point**."
4.  **Map to the Rating Rubric:** Use your analysis of the final verdict, pivotal language, and any conditions to assign the most fitting star rating based on the definitions below.

### **The 5-Star Rating Rubric**

**5 Stars: Unreserved Praise / Perfect Experience**
-   **Meaning:** The reviewer is thoroughly impressed and completely satisfied. The product met or surpassed all expectations without any significant drawbacks. The satisfaction is total.
-   **Litmus Test:** Does the review communicate complete delight, even without using hyperbole? A review stating, "This worked exactly as I hoped it would. I'm very pleased," is a 5-star review because there are no reservations.
-   **Signal Words:** *perfect, loved it, amazing, flawless, exactly what I wanted, life-saver, couldn't be happier*.

**4 Stars: Strongly Positive / The Standard "Good" Review**
-   **Meaning:** The default for a positive review. The user is happy, would recommend the product, and it performs its function well. It just lacks the pure, unqualified enthusiasm of a 5-star review. This can also apply to a great product with a very minor, easily overlooked issue.
-   **Litmus Test:** Is the overall sentiment clearly positive and recommending, but without declaring the product to be perfect or a game-changer?
-   **Signal Words:** *great, excellent, very good, highly recommend, works well, happy with purchase, good quality*.

**3 Stars: Mixed, Neutral, or Conditional / "It's Okay"**
-   **Meaning:** The experience was merely adequate or had a significant trade-off. This is the rating for reviews that are **balanced (pros and cons)**, **lukewarm ("it's fine")**, or where the recommendation is **conditional**.
-   **Litmus Test:** Does the review contain a major "but"? Is the praise weak or dependent on a specific circumstance (price, user type, etc.)?
-   **Classic 3-Star Scenarios:**
    -   A review balancing positives against significant negatives: "The screen is beautiful, but the battery life is a real problem."
    -   A review with heavily qualified praise: "This is a decent phone, **if you can get it on sale**."
    -   A lukewarm, analytical review: "It's an **interesting concept** and serves its purpose, but it **feels a bit unpolished**."
-   **Signal Words:** *okay, fine, decent, average, it works, has its pros and cons, not bad but...*.

**2 Stars: Significant Flaws / Disappointment**
-   **Meaning:** The overall tone is negative. While the product might be technically functional, it has major issues that seriously detract from the experience. The reviewer is disappointed and focuses on the product's shortcomings.
-   **Litmus Test:** Is the product usable, but the review's primary purpose is to complain about what's wrong with it?
-   **Signal Words:** *disappointed, frustrating, not great, too many issues, wouldn't buy again, barely functional*.

**1 Star: Critical Failure / Unusable or Misleading**
-   **Meaning:** The product is fundamentally broken, does not perform its core function, or was deceptively advertised. The review is a warning to other potential buyers. Any minor positive points are irrelevant in the face of the core failure.
-   **Litmus Test:** Does the reviewer state that the product is essentially useless for its intended purpose?
-   **Signal Words:** *useless, broken, scam, waste of money, returned it, do not buy, dangerous, false advertising*.
