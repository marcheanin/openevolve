        Here is a variation of the instruction that preserves the semantic meaning and logical flow but uses different phrasing, structure, and examples.

***

**Objective:** You are a zero-shot classifier. Your function is to analyze an Amazon product review and assign it a star rating from 1 to 5.

**Core Directive:** You must follow a hierarchical decision process. Evaluate the review against each rule in sequence. The first rule that provides a definitive rating is the final answer. Stop all further processing once a rating is determined.

### **Rating Decision Hierarchy**

---

### **Rule 1: Explicit Rating Extraction (Highest Priority)**

This rule overrides all others.

1.  **Scan for Direct Ratings:** Thoroughly examine the review text for any explicit mention of a rating score.
    *   **Examples:** "five stars," "I'd give it a 3/5," "rating: 2," "a perfect 10 out of 10," "one star review."
2.  **Normalize and Output:** If a direct rating is found, convert it to the 1-5 star scale (e.g., a score of "10/10" becomes 5 stars; "2/10" becomes 1 star). This number is your final output.
3.  **Halt Execution:** Cease all further analysis.

---

### **Rule 2: Catastrophic Failure Detection (Second Highest Priority)**

Execute this rule only if Rule 1 does not apply.

1.  **Identify Critical Flaws:** Determine if the review describes a catastrophic failure of the product.
2.  **Definition of Catastrophic Failure:** The product is non-functional, arrived broken, is fundamentally unsafe, or its core advertised purpose is completely unachievable.
    *   **Examples:** "The device was dead on arrival," "the chair collapsed under normal weight," "the software is unusable due to a persistent bug," "this is a counterfeit item," "the vacuum has zero suction."
3.  **Assign Rating:** If a catastrophic failure is identified, the rating is unequivocally **1 Star**. This holds true even if the reviewer mentions trivial positives (e.g., "fast shipping, but the camera won't turn on").
4.  **Halt Execution:** Cease all further analysis.

---

### **Rule 3: Qualitative Sentiment Analysis (Default Rule)**

If and only if the review passes through Rule 1 and Rule 2 without a rating, apply the following sentiment rubric. The goal is to gauge the *degree* of satisfaction, not just positive vs. negative.

**Sentiment-to-Star Mapping:**

**5 Stars: Unqualified Enthusiasm / Perfection**
*   **Meaning:** The reviewer expresses overwhelming satisfaction, often using superlatives to frame the product as flawless or the best in its class. It's an absolute, unreserved endorsement.
*   **Litmus Test:** Does the reviewer imply the product has no significant flaws and stands above all others?
*   **Telltale Phrases:** *best I've ever owned, game-changer, flawless, absolutely perfect, exceeded my wildest dreams, couldn't be happier*.
*   **Key Distinction:** A review that is strongly positive but lacks this superlative quality (e.g., "it's a great product") is 4 stars, not 5.

**4 Stars: Strong Positive / High Satisfaction**
*   **Meaning:** This is the default for a very positive review. The product delivered on its promises, works well, and the customer is happy and would recommend it. The praise is strong but not absolute.
*   **Litmus Test:** Is the reviewer happy and recommending the product, but stops short of calling it "perfect" or the "best ever"?
*   **Telltale Phrases:** *excellent, great, very pleased, works perfectly, highly recommended, no issues, solid purchase*.

**3 Stars: Mixed or Moderate Satisfaction / "It's Adequate"**
*   **Meaning:** The product is functional but not impressive. The review often highlights a balance of pros and cons, or expresses a general feeling of indifference. The product simply met the bare minimum expectations.
*   **Litmus Test:** Is the product functional but unexciting, or are its positive aspects offset by notable drawbacks?
*   **Telltale Phrases:** *it's okay, decent, does the job, average, not bad but not great, has some flaws*.

**2 Stars: Significant Dissatisfaction / Major Flaws**
*   **Meaning:** The product is a source of frustration and disappointment due to significant, but not catastrophic, flaws. It may technically function, but it performs poorly or fails to meet key expectations, leading to regret.
*   **Litmus Test:** Is the core message one of frustration and warning due to major, but not catastrophic, flaws?
*   **Telltale Phrases:** *disappointing, poor quality, frustrating, not as described, wouldn't buy again, too many issues*.

**1 Star: Complete Dissatisfaction / Warning to Others**
*   **Meaning:** This rating is for reviews that did not trigger Rule 2 but still convey a sense of total failure or disgust. The product is considered a waste of money, and the review's primary intent is to warn others away from making the same mistake.
*   **Litmus Test:** Is the review's primary intent to warn other potential buyers away from a product the reviewer considers worthless?
*   **Telltale Phrases:** *awful, garbage, useless, avoid at all costs, returned it immediately, complete scam*.
