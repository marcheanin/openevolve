**Objective: Infer a 1-to-5 Star Rating from a Customer Review**

Your function is to analyze the text of a product review and assign it a star rating from 1 (lowest) to 5 (highest). Your evaluation should focus on the reviewer's ultimate conclusion and the intensity of their sentiment.

### **Step 1: Check for Explicit Numerical Ratings (Highest Priority)**

This is a non-negotiable first step that overrides any sentiment analysis.

Before analyzing the text's tone, you must first search the review for any direct mention of a rating.
-   **Examples include:** "I'd give this 3 stars," "5 out of 5," "a 2/10 experience," "one-star product."
-   If you find such a number, it is the authoritative rating. Normalize this score to a 1-to-5 star scale (e.g., a "2/10" becomes 1 star, a "9/10" becomes 5 stars) and provide it as the answer. No further analysis is required.

### **Step 2: Analyze Sentiment if No Explicit Rating Exists**

If the review does not contain a direct numerical rating, you must deduce the star rating by analyzing the text.

**Core Principle: Focus on the Reviewer's Overall Takeaway and Enthusiasm**
Your task is to determine the reviewer's final judgment. Are they unequivocally thrilled? Are they satisfied but not ecstatic? Do they present a balanced view with pros and cons? Or is their primary goal to issue a warning? The details within the review serve to justify this final takeaway.

**A Note on Context:** Always interpret language based on the overall tone. For instance, in an otherwise glowing review, a phrase like "the plot was just sick" is high praise (using slang for "amazing"), not a criticism.

---

### **Rating Scale Breakdown**

**5 Stars: Outstanding and Enthusiastic**
-   **Meaning:** The reviewer is thoroughly delighted, and the product or experience surpassed their expectations. The praise is strong, specific, and lacks any significant reservations. This rating signifies a memorable or "wow" experience.
-   **Ask Yourself:** Does the reviewer sound genuinely excited or deeply satisfied? Is the positive sentiment overwhelming and unqualified?
-   **Note:** A 5-star review doesn't require hyperbole. "A truly wonderful book. The writing was beautiful and the story was compelling from start to finish" is a 5-star review due to its strong, unreserved praise.
-   **Common Indicators:** *loved it, perfect, incredible, best I've ever used, exceeded my expectations, phenomenal, couldn't ask for more, highly recommend, a must-buy*.

**4 Stars: Very Good and Satisfied**
-   **Meaning:** This is the quintessential positive review. The user is happy, the product performed well, and they would recommend it. It's a strong endorsement that lacks the exceptional enthusiasm or "wow" factor of a 5-star rating.
-   **Ask Yourself:** Is the review clearly positive and the user satisfied, but the praise is more standard and less effusive?
-   **Note:** This is the correct rating for most solid, positive feedback.
-   **Common Indicators:** *great product, works well, very good, happy with my purchase, no issues, does the job, solid, excellent*. A review like "Excellent phone. The camera is great and the battery life is good" is a classic 4-star review.

**3 Stars: Average / Mixed Feelings**
-   **Meaning:** The experience was simply adequate, or the reviewer has mixed feelings. The review often **explicitly weighs notable pros against notable cons**. The praise is lukewarm or qualified.
-   **Ask Yourself:** Does the review read like a list of pros and cons? Is the overall tone neutral or unenthusiastic?
-   **Common Indicators:** *it's okay, decent, average, fine for the price, has some good and bad points, not bad but not great, adequate*.

**2 Stars: Negative and Disappointing**
-   **Meaning:** The overall sentiment is negative. While the product may technically work on some level, it has significant flaws, failed to meet important expectations, or was a major hassle. The negative aspects dominate the review.
-   **Ask Yourself:** Is the main point of the review to communicate disappointment and highlight significant problems?
-   **Common Indicators:** *disappointed, poor quality, too many issues, wouldn't buy again, frustrating, barely functional, not as described*.

**1 Star: Total Failure / Does Not Work**
-   **Meaning:** This rating is reserved for products that are fundamentally flawed or non-functional. They fail at their primary purpose, are broken, deceptive, or even dangerous. The review serves as a strong warning to other potential buyers.
-   **Ask Yourself:** Does the review state that the product is unusable for its main function? This single factor overrides any minor positive points mentioned.
-   **Examples of Core Failure:** A heater that doesn't produce heat; a digital book with missing chapters; a tool that breaks on its first use.
-   **Common Indicators:** *useless, junk, waste of money, do not buy, broken, scam, returned immediately, dangerous, completely fails*.
