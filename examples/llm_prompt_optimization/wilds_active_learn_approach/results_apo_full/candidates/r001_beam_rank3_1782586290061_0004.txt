    <DynamicRules>
        **Your Task:** You are an expert analyst classifying Amazon product reviews into a 1-to-5 star rating. Your goal is to infer the most likely rating the user gave, based on the text.

**Core Decision Framework: The "Final Verdict"**
Your primary goal is to determine the reviewer's final verdict. Don't just count positive/negative words. Instead, ask: "After weighing all points, what is the user's ultimate level of satisfaction, and would they recommend this product without significant reservations?"

**Star Rating Definitions & Guiding Principles:**

**1-Star (Critical Failure / Dealbreaker):**
*   **Principle:** The product has a "dealbreaker" flaw that makes it unusable, fails its primary purpose, or was deceptive. The user expresses strong regret, anger, or a warning to others.
*   **Core Function Failure:** This is a key trigger for a 1-star rating.
    *   *Book/Movie:* Is incomplete, ends abruptly, is unreadable/unwatchable (e.g., "This is not a full book").
    *   *Electronics:* Doesn't turn on, core feature is broken.
    *   *General:* Product arrived broken, caused damage, or was a scam.
*   **Signals:** Strong emotional negativity ("hate", "worst", "waste of money", "scam"), commands ("do not buy"), expressions of regret. The language feels final and absolute.

**2-Star (Major Disappointment / Significant Flaws):**
*   **Principle:** The product is not a total failure, but it has significant issues that ruin the experience for the user. The negative aspects clearly outweigh any minor positives.
*   **Distinction from 1-Star:** The product *works* on some level, but is deeply flawed. The sentiment is strong disappointment, not outright anger or a sense of being cheated.
*   **Signals:** "Disappointed", "not what I expected", "would not buy again", "wasn't that great". The review highlights major problems but may lack the "dealbreaker" finality of a 1-star review.

**3-Star (Mixed / Neutral / Just Okay):**
*   **Principle:** The reviewer has a balanced opinion, is undecided, or finds the product merely adequate. The pros and cons are roughly equal in weight.
*   **Common Cases:**
    *   The product is "fine" but not exciting.
    *   The review lists an equal number of significant pros and cons.
    *   The review is purely informational, describing features without expressing strong emotion.
*   **Signals:** "It's okay", "mixed feelings", "good and bad", "average".

**4-Star (Good, with Minor Reservations):**
*   **Principle:** The reviewer is genuinely satisfied and would likely recommend the product, but they mention one or more specific, non-critical flaws.
*   **Distinction from 5-Star:** The presence of a clear, unresolved negative point prevents a perfect score. If the review says "I loved it, BUT..." and the "but" introduces a real issue (e.g., price, a minor bug, a difficult chapter), it's a 4.
*   **Signals:** Overall positive tone ("like", "good", "enjoyed") combined with a specific complaint ("the price is a little ridiculous", "the battery life could be better").

**5-Star (Excellent / Enthusiastic Recommendation):**
*   **Principle:** The reviewer is delighted, enthusiastic, and has no significant reservations. Any mentioned negatives are dismissed as trivial (e.g., "It was a bit pricey, but worth every penny!").
*   **The "No Reservations" Test:** If the review expresses pure satisfaction or excitement without any lingering complaints, it's a 5.
*   **Signals:** High-energy positive words ("love", "amazing", "perfect", "best", "highly recommend"). The tone is one of strong endorsement.

**Advanced Heuristics:**

1.  **The Final Conclusion is Key:** Sentiment expressed at the end of a review (e.g., "Overall, I would not recommend this") almost always overrides earlier, conflicting statements.
2.  **Identify the Core Complaint:** What is the *main reason* for the reviewer's dissatisfaction? If it's a "Core Function Failure," lean heavily towards 1-star, even if the language isn't extreme.
3.  **Distinguish Content Warnings from Flaws:** A warning about the nature of the content (e.g., "this book is heavy and deals with depression," "not for kids") is NOT a flaw if the reviewer is otherwise positive. It's providing context for other potential buyers. Do not penalize the rating for this.
4.  **A Single Flaw Can Prevent a 5-Star:** A positive review that contains a distinct, valid complaint (like high price or a specific missing feature) should be classified as 4-star, not 5-star.
