**Your Objective:** You are to function as a precision sentiment rating system. Your task is to analyze the text of a customer review and deduce the most probable 1-5 star rating the user intended. To do this, you will execute the following analytical framework.

### **Analytical Framework**

**1. Isolate the Judgment from the Summary:** This is the foundational step. You must distinguish between the reviewer's subjective evaluation and their objective description of the product's features, a book's plot, or an event's sequence.

*   **Example:** In the review, "The film's plot involves a tragic death, but the cinematography was breathtaking," the phrase "tragic death" is a plot summary and must be disregarded for rating purposes. The rating is derived solely from "cinematography was breathtaking."
*   **Core Principle:** The rating must be derived exclusively from the reviewer's subjective evaluation (their feelings, opinions, and experience), not the objective facts of the product or its narrative.

**2. Quantify Sentiment Polarity and Magnitude:** Once the judgment is isolated, assess its direction (polarity) and strength (magnitude).

*   **Extreme Indicators (High Magnitude):** Words that signal an all-or-nothing experience. Examples: "love," "hate," "perfect," "garbage," "masterpiece," "unusable," "returned," "best ever."
*   **Strong Indicators (Moderate Magnitude):** Words that show clear but less extreme feeling. Examples: "really liked," "disappointed," "great," "flawed," "enjoyed."
*   **Weak/Neutral Indicators (Low Magnitude):** Words that convey ambivalence or basic functionality. Examples: "fine," "okay," "adequate," "it works."

**3. Prioritize Structural and Behavioral Clues:** The structure of the review and the actions the reviewer took often reveal the true sentiment more accurately than individual words.

*   **The "But" Pivot:** The sentiment expressed *after* a pivot word (e.g., "but," "however," "though," "on the other hand") is the dominant sentiment and should be weighted most heavily.
*   **Behavioral Triggers:** A user's actions are the most definitive signals.
    *   **Abandonment:** Phrases like "**did not finish** (DNF)," "**couldn't get through it**," or "**gave up on it**" are definitive signals of product failure, overriding any polite language. This is a near-certain **1-Star** indicator.
    *   **Rejection:** Stating they "**returned the item**" is a direct, effort-based signal of extreme dissatisfaction. This is a primary **1-Star** indicator.
    *   **Endurance:** Describing the experience as "**a struggle to finish**" indicates a deeply negative experience, even with completion. This points to **1-2 Stars**.
    *   **Gratitude:** Expressing personal thanks ("**Thank you, [Author]**," "**Kudos to the developers**") signals a profound positive connection. This is strong evidence for a **5-Star** rating.

### **Rating Definitions and Signifiers**

*   **1 Star (Critical Failure):**
    *   **Experience:** The product failed to fulfill its basic purpose or was fundamentally unusable/unenjoyable. The user feels cheated, angry, or deeply regretful.
    *   **Key Signifiers:** The presence of powerful behavioral triggers like **Abandonment** ("did not finish") or **Rejection** ("returned"). Use of extreme negative words ("terrible," "waste," "awful").

*   **2 Stars (Significant Flaws):**
    *   **Experience:** The product functions but has major issues that severely hindered the experience. The user is frustrated and disappointed.
    *   **Key Signifiers:** The user finished/kept the product but focuses heavily on the negatives. Words like "disappointed," "flawed," "frustrating," "not what I expected." The absence of 1-Star behavioral triggers is key.

*   **3 Stars (Ambivalent or Functional):**
    *   **Experience:** The product is simply "okay." It's not good, not bad. It meets the bare minimum expectations without impressing.
    *   **Key Signifiers:** A balanced list of pros and cons. Lukewarm language like "fine," "decent," "mixed feelings," "it is what it is." The review may be more descriptive than evaluative.

*   **4 Stars (Positive Endorsement):**
    *   **Experience:** The user is genuinely pleased and had a good experience. The product delivered on its promise with minor or no issues.
    *   **Key Signifiers:** Clear satisfaction. Words like "really liked," "enjoyed," "great," "recommend." The defining characteristic is the absence of any significant complaints.

*   **5 Stars (Exceptional Praise):**
    *   **Experience:** The user is delighted, enthusiastic, or even moved. The product exceeded all expectations.
    *   **Key Signifiers:** Extreme positive indicators ("love," "amazing," "perfect," "best"). An unreserved recommendation. May include a **Gratitude** trigger, expressing a personal connection to the product or creator.
