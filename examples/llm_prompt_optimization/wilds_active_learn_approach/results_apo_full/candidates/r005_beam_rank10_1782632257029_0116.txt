**Objective: Rate Product Reviews on a 1-5 Star Scale**

Your function is to analyze a given product review and assign it a star rating from 1 (lowest) to 5 (highest). Your goal is to accurately determine the reviewer's overall judgment by interpreting the context, tone, and specific details of their feedback.

### **Primary Directive: Identify and Apply Explicit Ratings**

This directive takes precedence over all subsequent sentiment analysis.

1.  **Initial Scan:** Before any other analysis, your first step is to scan the review for any explicit numerical rating or star value.
    *   Examples include: "I'd give this 4.5 stars", "2 out of 5", "a perfect 10/10", "one-star product".
2.  **Immediate Assignment:** If an explicit rating is present, it is the definitive answer. Normalize this rating to the 1-5 star system (e.g., a 10/10 becomes 5 stars, a 4.5/5 rounds up to 5 stars) and output that value. No further analysis is required.

### **Secondary Protocol: Sentiment-Based Rating**

If the review contains no explicit rating, you will perform a comprehensive sentiment analysis using the following guidelines.

**Guiding Principle: Prioritize Overall Judgment, Not Keyword Counting**
Your analysis should focus on the reviewer's final verdict. A review filled with positive adjectives can still result in a low rating if the product failed in a key area. Conversely, a review that lists flaws can still earn a high rating if the reviewer concludes it's a fantastic value.

**Analysis Workflow:**

1.  **Gauge the Dominant Emotion:** Read the full text to understand the primary sentiment—is it delight, satisfaction, indifference, disappointment, or anger?
2.  **Analyze Contrast and Qualification:** Look for pivotal words like "but," "however," "although," and "despite." The information following these words often carries more weight and reveals the reviewer's conclusive opinion.
3.  **Weight the Final Verdict:** The concluding sentences are critical. This is typically where the user summarizes their experience and gives their final recommendation (or warning).
4.  **Map to Rating Criteria:** Use the detailed definitions below to match the review's overall sentiment to the most appropriate star rating.

---

### **Star Rating Criteria**

**5 Stars: Outstanding / Strong Endorsement**
-   **Hallmarks:** The reviewer is highly enthusiastic, and the product either exceeded their expectations or represents exceptional value for its price. This is the rating for a "perfect find," not necessarily a "perfect product."
-   **Value Context:** A review can point out minor shortcomings but still award 5 stars if the overall value is perceived as exceptional ("a steal for the price," "outperforms expensive brands"). The final feeling is one of being thoroughly pleased with the purchase.
-   **Indicators:** *love it, amazing, perfect for my needs, exceeded my expectations, best I've tried, incredible value, highly recommend without reservation*.

**4 Stars: Good / Generally Satisfied**
-   **Hallmarks:** The reviewer is happy with the product, it performs as expected, and they would recommend it to others. The review is clearly positive but lacks the overwhelming enthusiasm or "exceeded expectations" sentiment of a 5-star rating.
-   **Distinguishing from 5 Stars:** This is the standard for a solid, positive review. If a review uses terms like "great," "works well," or "very good" and mentions a small, manageable issue without the "great value" context, it's a 4.
-   **Indicators:** *great product, works well, I like it, happy with my purchase, would recommend, very good*.

**3 Stars: Average / Mixed Feelings**
-   **Hallmarks:** The product is adequate but not impressive. The review often presents a balanced view with significant pros and cons, resulting in a lukewarm recommendation or ambivalence. Think of this as the "it's okay" rating.
-   **The "Shrug" Test:** Does the review feel non-committal? A review that simply lists features without praise, or one that concludes with "it's decent" or "it has its flaws," falls into this category.
-   **Indicators:** *it's okay, decent, average, does the job, has pros and cons, not bad but not great*.

**2 Stars: Below Average / Disappointed**
-   **Hallmarks:** The reviewer's experience was predominantly negative. The product has major issues, failed to meet basic expectations, or caused frustration. The negative aspects clearly outweigh any positives mentioned.
-   **Distinguishing from 3 Stars:** A 3-star review balances good and bad. A 2-star review is dominated by the bad; the user feels let down and would not advise others to buy the product.
-   **Indicators:** *disappointing, not very good, frustrating, too many issues, probably wouldn't buy again*.

**1 Star: Defective / Do Not Buy**
-   **Hallmarks:** The product was a complete failure, or the experience left the reviewer feeling angry or ripped off. The primary purpose of the review is to warn other potential buyers.
-   **Core Function Failure Rule:** If a review states that the product's **primary purpose is defeated** (e.g., "arrived broken," "doesn't turn on," "crashed constantly," "unusable"), the rating **MUST be 1 Star**. This is true even if the reviewer notes a minor positive, like "the color was nice."
-   **Indicators:** *terrible, garbage, waste of money, do not buy, returned it, broken on arrival, scam, useless*.
