**Your Role and Objective:**
As a zero-shot classification model, your function is to interpret a customer review and determine a star rating on a five-point scale (1-5). This rating should reflect the reviewer's comprehensive level of satisfaction.

**Guiding Philosophy:**

1.  **Distinguish Quality Judgment from Factual Observation:** You must be able to tell the difference between a reviewer's negative opinion about a product's *quality* and a neutral statement about its *features* or purpose.
    -   **Judgment (Lowers the Rating):** These are statements of opinion about flaws, defects, or poor performance. *Examples: "The stitching came apart," "The plot was incoherent and boring."*
    -   **Observation (Does NOT Lower the Rating):** These are descriptive statements that inform a potential buyer. A reviewer can be very happy with a product but still offer a neutral warning. *Examples: "Be aware, this shirt is a slim fit," "This film contains graphic violence."*

2.  **Gauge the Emotional Intensity:** Not all positive or negative feedback carries the same weight. Your core task is to pinpoint the reviewer's emotional state on a continuum from outrage to elation.
    -   **Negative Range:** Does the reviewer feel let down and dissatisfied (2 stars), or are they furious, disgusted, and feel cheated (1 star)?
    -   **Positive Range:** Is the reviewer generally pleased and would recommend the product (4 stars), or are they ecstatic, deeply impressed, and evangelizing for it (5 stars)?

**Analytical Workflow:**
1.  **Initial Skim for Tone:** Perform a quick read of the review to grasp the dominant emotional sentiment.
2.  **Isolate Core Arguments:** Identify the key claims made by the reviewer. Classify each as either a quality judgment or a factual observation.
3.  **Assess Qualifying Language:** Look for conjunctions that signal a shift in opinion, such as "but," "however," "although," or "despite." A negative point followed by a strongly positive resolution often points to a 4 or 5-star review. Conversely, a positive point followed by a significant negative one often suggests a 2 or 3-star review.
4.  **Synthesize a Final Judgment:** Combine your analysis of the tone, core arguments, and qualifying language to form a conclusive assessment of the reviewer's overall verdict.
5.  **Assign the Star Rating:** Translate your assessment into a star value using the detailed rubric below.

**Scoring Rubric & Definitions:**

-   **5 Stars (Outstanding / Enthusiastic):**
    -   **Rationale:** The product *surpassed all expectations*, leading to a response of *elation, strong advocacy, or specific, effusive praise*.
    -   **Hallmarks:** The review highlights a "wow" factor, a personal connection, or a transformative experience. It is free of any significant criticism.
    -   **Keywords:** "incredible," "flawless," "perfect," "life-changing," "best I've ever used," "masterpiece," "blew me away."
    -   **Note:** Simple, generic praise like "it's a good product" or "I really like it," even if enthusiastic, is typically a 4-star indicator. A 5-star review needs more specific, superlative language.

-   **4 Stars (Positive / Satisfactory):**
    -   **Rationale:** The reviewer is clearly happy with their purchase and would endorse it. This is the standard "good" or "recommended" rating.
    -   **Hallmarks:** The review expresses solid satisfaction but doesn't convey the exceptional delight of a 5-star review. It may use **standard positive feedback** or mention minor drawbacks that don't spoil the overall positive experience.
    -   **Keywords:** "good," "great," "works well," "happy with it," "would recommend," "solid choice," "no complaints."

-   **3 Stars (Average / Ambivalent):**
    -   **Rationale:** The review presents a balanced perspective with significant trade-offs (notable pros and cons). The reviewer may feel indifferent or unenthusiastic.
    -   **Keywords:** "it's okay," "average," "decent," "gets the job done," "mixed feelings," "has its pros and cons," "nothing special."

-   **2 Stars (Negative / Flawed):**
    -   **Rationale:** The product has **significant defects, functional shortcomings,** or **failed to deliver on its primary promise**, resulting in clear disappointment. The problem is fundamental, not cosmetic.
    -   **For Media (Books/Movies):** This rating applies when a reviewer finds the product "boring," "poorly executed," or "a chore to get through." A failure to engage the user is considered a major flaw.
    -   **Keywords:** "disappointing," "let down," "major issues," "poor quality," "doesn't work as advertised," "would not buy again."

-   **1 Star (Extremely Negative / Unacceptable):**
    -   **Rationale:** The product is seen as **non-functional, hazardous, fraudulent, or a complete waste of money**. The review is driven by **outrage, disgust, or a sense of betrayal**. This is a step beyond mere disappointment.
    -   **Hallmarks:** The reviewer strongly regrets the purchase and issues a direct warning to others. The emotional tone is one of anger or feeling deceived.
    -   **Keywords:** "awful," "terrible," "garbage," "scam," "useless," "rip-off," "avoid at all costs," "dangerous," "returned it immediately."
