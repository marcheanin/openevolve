**Core Philosophy: Judge the Forest, Not the Trees**
Avoid the trap of simply counting positive or negative words. Your analysis must capture the reviewer's final, holistic impression. **Detailed Star Rating Definitions:**

*   **5 Stars (Unreserved Enthusiasm / Exceeded Expectations):**
    *   **Essence:** The product or experience was not just good, it was exceptional, flawless, or deeply moving. It went far beyond the reviewer's expectations, creating a "wow" moment.
    *   **Litmus Test:** Does the review communicate a sense of surprise, delight, or a strong personal connection? Is the recommendation passionate and without any reservations?
    *   **Key Indicators:**
        *   **Superlatives:** "Perfect," "flawless," "best ever," "changed my life," "a masterpiece," "exceeded every expectation."
        *   **Profound Impact:** Especially for media/art, look for expressions of gratitude or personal resonance (e.g., "This book spoke to my soul," "Thank you for creating this"). This signals a level of impact beyond mere enjoyment.
        *   **The 4 vs. 5 Distinction:** A list of strong positive adjectives ("great," "awesome," "wonderful") describes a **4-star** review. To be a 5, it needs that extra layer of being "blown away," personally touched, or genuinely surprised by the quality.

*   **4 Stars (Strongly Positive / The Standard "Great" Review):**
    *   **Essence:** This is the default rating for a product the user genuinely liked and would recommend. They are happy and satisfied. The product met their high expectations but didn't necessarily provide a life-altering experience.
    *   **Litmus Test:** Is this a clear and solid "thumbs-up" review that expresses satisfaction without the overwhelming emotion of a 5-star rating?
    *   **Key Indicators:**
        *   **The Home of "Great":** This is the proper category for terms like "excellent," "fantastic," "awesome," "loved it," "highly recommend," and "very good." A long, detailed review full of such praise is the quintessential 4-star review.
        *   **Enthusiastic but Grounded:** Analytical reviews from fans that are full of praise are often 4 stars. They are appreciating the work, not declaring it objectively perfect for all time.
        *   **Short & Positive:** Simple, direct praise like "Works great, no complaints!" or "Really enjoyed this book" are classic 4-star examples.

*   **3 Stars (Mixed or Lukewarm / The "It's Fine" Review):**
    *   **Essence:** The product was adequate but not impressive. The reviewer might present a balanced list of pros and cons, or their praise might feel faint and unenthusiastic. The overall feeling is "meh" or "it did the job."
    *   **Litmus Test:** Does the review feel more like a polite nod than a genuine endorsement? Is the praise qualified or tepid?
    *   **Key Indicators:** "It's okay," "decent," "fine for the price," "not bad." **Pay close attention to phrases like "good book," "well-written," or "decent read," as these often signal a 3-star rating when they are the main takeaway.**

*   **2 Stars (Generally Negative / Significant Dissatisfaction):**
    *   **Essence:** The reviewer is clearly unhappy. The product had major flaws, failed to meet basic expectations, and is not something they would recommend. The negative aspects are the main story.
    *   **Litmus Test:** Do the cons clearly outweigh the pros? Is the primary emotion one of disappointment?
    *   **Key Indicators:** "Disappointing," "not what I expected," "too many problems," "wouldn't recommend," "a real letdown."

*   **1 Star (Overwhelmingly Negative / Product Failure):**
    *   **Essence:** A complete failure of the product or a user experience that sparked anger and regret. The reviewer feels they wasted their money and is actively warning others to stay away.
    *   **Litmus Test:** Is the tone angry or regretful? Is the primary purpose of the review to warn others?
    *   **Key Indicators:** "Awful," "terrible," "garbage," "waste of money," "returned it immediately," "DO NOT BUY."

**The Non-Functional Override:**
*   If a review states that a product arrived broken, was unusable for its main purpose (e.g., a kettle that doesn't boil water, a book with pages missing), or simply did not work, it should be classified as **1 Star**, even if the reviewer mentions something minor they liked (e.g., "the color was nice, but it arrived in pieces").
