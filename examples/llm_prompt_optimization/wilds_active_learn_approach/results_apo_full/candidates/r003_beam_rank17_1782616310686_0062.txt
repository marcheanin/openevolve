### Variation 1 (More Direct & Action-Oriented)

**Your Mission:** Analyze the text of an Amazon product review and assign the most probable 1-5 star rating it originally had.

**Priority One: Identify Explicit Ratings**
Before any other analysis, you must search for explicit mentions of a rating. This is your primary instruction and overrides all other steps.
1.  **Numerical Ratings:** Find any numbers like "4 out of 5," "I'd rate this a 2," or "9/10." Convert these to the 1-5 star scale.
2.  **Rating Commentary:** Look for phrases where the user discusses the rating itself, such as "This product deserves 5 stars" or "I don't understand the low ratings, this is at least a 4." These are powerful indicators.

**If No Explicit Rating is Found: Execute Sentiment Analysis Protocol**

1.  **Initial Assessment:** Read the full review for a general sense of its tone—is it positive, negative, or balanced?
2.  **Weigh the Conclusion:** The final sentences often contain the user's ultimate judgment. Give these extra weight.
3.  **Note the Turnaround:** Identify contrast words like "but," "however," or "while." The sentiment that follows these words is typically the dominant one.
4.  **Match to Rating Profile:** Use the detailed profiles below to assign the final star rating.

**Star Rating Profiles:**

*   **5 Stars (Peak Satisfaction / Glowing Praise):** The review is an unreserved, enthusiastic recommendation.
    *   **Triggers for a 5:** Assign a 5 if you find **any** of these:
        *   **Highest Praise:** Uses superlatives like "perfect," "best," "flawless," "life-changing."
        *   **Strong Repurchase Intent:** States a clear intention to buy again, get the next in the series, or explore more from the brand/author (e.g., "Can't wait for book 2," "I'm buying another one for my mom").
        *   **Purely Positive:** The review is 100% positive with no drawbacks mentioned, filled with words like "amazing," "loved it," "excellent."

*   **4 Stars (Solidly Positive / High Satisfaction):** The user is very happy and recommends the product, but lacks the "wow" factor of a 5-star review. This is the default for a "great" review that isn't ecstatic or doesn't mention repurchase intent.

*   **3 Stars (Mixed / Average / Fine):** Represents a clear trade-off of good and bad points ("I liked X, but I hated Y"). It can also describe an underwhelming or simply "okay" experience.

*   **2 Stars (Negative / Disappointing):** The overall feeling is negative. The product has major flaws and failed to meet expectations, though it might have a minor positive aspect. The negatives clearly overshadow any positives.

*   **1 Star (Total Failure / Warning):** The product was broken, unusable, or fundamentally misrepresented its purpose. The user expresses anger, regret, or actively warns others not to buy it ("waste of money," "avoid at all costs," "returned for a refund").

**Exclusion Rule:**
*   Disregard boilerplate text such as "I received this product for free in exchange for an honest review" or "ARC Copy." This is not part of the user's opinion.

### Variation 2 (Framed as a Heuristic Algorithm)

**Objective:** To function as an expert system for deducing the 1-5 star rating from an Amazon review text. Follow this algorithm precisely.

**Step 1: Scan for Explicit Rating Data (Override Condition)**
`IF` the review text contains a direct rating (e.g., "3/5", "7 out of 10") OR commentary on what a rating should be (e.g., "deserves 5 stars", "should have higher than 2 stars")...
`THEN` convert this to a 1-5 rating and terminate the process. This is your final output.
`ELSE` (if no explicit data is found), proceed to Step 2.

**Step 2: Deep Sentiment Analysis**

*   **2a. Initial Pass:** Perform a holistic read to establish the overall sentiment (Positive, Negative, Mixed).
*   **2b. Final Verdict Analysis:** Isolate the concluding statements, as they typically summarize the user's definitive opinion.
*   **2c. Contrast Keyword Detection:** Flag pivot words ("but", "however", "unfortunately"). The clause following the pivot holds dominant sentiment.
*   **2d. Apply Classification Model:** Based on the analysis, map the sentiment to a star rating using the criteria below.

**Classification Model (Star Rating Definitions):**

*   **5-Star Condition (Enthusiastic Endorsement):** Assign 5 stars if **ANY** of the following are true:
    1.  Contains superlative language ("perfect," "best ever," "flawless").
    2.  Expresses enthusiastic future intent ("will buy again," "can't wait for the next one").
    3.  Is entirely positive with no reservations and uses strong, excited language ("amazing," "incredible," "loved it").

*   **4-Star Condition (Strongly Positive):** Assign 4 stars if the review is strongly positive but does not meet any of the specific 5-star conditions. It's a "great" or "excellent" product with high satisfaction, but without the ecstatic tone or future commitment.

*   **3-Star Condition (Balanced/Neutral):** Assign 3 stars if the review presents a clear balance of significant pros and cons, or describes the product as merely "okay," "average," or "fine."

*   **2-Star Condition (Predominantly Negative):** Assign 2 stars if the dominant sentiment is negative and the product is described as disappointing or having significant flaws, but isn't a complete failure. The user would not recommend it.

*   **1-Star Condition (Complete Dissatisfaction):** Assign 1 star if the review indicates one of the following:
    1.  **Critical Failure:** The product was broken, didn't work, or was unusable.
    2.  **Misrepresentation:** The product was the opposite of its advertised purpose.
    3.  **Strong Negative Emotion:** The user expresses anger, regret, or warns others away ("terrible," "waste of money," "do not buy").

**System Note:**
*   Filter out and ignore non-review text, such as ARC disclaimers or promotional statements.
