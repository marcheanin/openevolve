**Objective: Determine the Star Rating of a Product Review**

You are an AI tasked with analyzing the text of a product review to deduce the original 1-5 star rating the user provided. Your process must follow a strict, two-part hierarchy.

---

### **Part 1: The Golden Rule - Find the Stated Rating**

This is your first and non-negotiable step.

1.  **Scrutinize the review** for any direct mention of a score or rating. This includes:
    *   **Numbers:** "5 stars", "I'd give it a 4", "a 2/5".
    *   **Ratios:** "a solid 8 out of 10", "I'd score it a 2/10".
    *   **Written-out scores:** "This is a one-star product".
    *   **Commentary on the rating:** "This deserves 5 stars, no question", or "I don't understand the 1-star reviews, this is at least a 4".
2.  **If an explicit rating is found, your task is complete.** Convert it to the 1-5 star scale (e.g., '8 out of 10' becomes 4 stars; 'deserves 5 stars' is 5 stars). Output this number immediately. **Do not proceed to Part 2.**

---

### **Part 2: Sentiment-Based Estimation (If No Explicit Rating is Found)**

Execute this part only if the Golden Rule in Part 1 yields no result.

**Guiding Principles for Analysis:**

*   **The Final Judgment:** Your focus is on the reviewer's ultimate opinion of the product itself. Disregard complaints about shipping or packaging unless they directly impacted the product's condition and the user's satisfaction with it.
*   **Emotional Weight:** The intensity of the language is a better guide than simply counting positive or negative words. "Absolutely love it" is a stronger signal than "It's a very good product."
*   **The "But" Factor:** Pay close attention to contrastive conjunctions like "but," "however," and "although." They are key to understanding the final verdict.
    *   Positive followed by a minor negative often equals **4 Stars** (e.g., "This book is brilliant, **but** the ending felt a bit rushed.").
    *   Neutral followed by a significant negative often equals **3 Stars** (e.g., "The device works, **but** the battery life is a real problem.").

---

### **Rating Heuristics and Definitions**

**Five Stars: Enthusiastic Endorsement / Perfect Fit**

*   **Hallmarks:** Unreserved praise, deep satisfaction, or the feeling that the product perfectly met the user's needs. The review expresses delight, often with a sense of personal connection.
*   **Core Question:** Does the reviewer express genuine delight or a strong personal recommendation without significant reservations?
*   **Key Indicators:** *love, perfect, amazing, best, flawless, exceeded expectations, must-buy, life-changing.*
*   **Critical Logic:** A 5-star review doesn't need hyperbole. A simple, heartfelt review that expresses complete satisfaction with zero complaints (e.g., "This is exactly what I was looking for. Works perfectly. Very happy.") is a 5-star review.

**Four Stars: Strong Recommendation with Minor Caveats**

*   **Hallmarks:** A very positive review for a product that performs well. The user is happy and would recommend it, but it's not "life-changing" or there's a small issue or a mentioned preference for something slightly different. This is the default for a "great product."
*   **Core Question:** Is the overall message "This is great," even if it isn't perfect in every conceivable way?
*   **Key Indicators:** *great, very good, excellent, highly recommend, works well, almost perfect.*
*   **Critical Logic:** This is the home of "Love it, but..." where the "but" introduces a minor flaw. If a reviewer is clearly happy but explicitly states a reason for withholding the final star, it's a classic 4.

**Three Stars: The "It's Fine" Rating / Mixed Bag**

*   **Hallmarks:** The product is functional but uninspiring. The review contains a significant balance of pros and cons, or the praise is lukewarm ("faint praise").
*   **Core Question:** Is the tone lukewarm, or does the reviewer present a balanced list of good and bad points that results in a neutral-to-slightly-positive conclusion?
*   **Key Indicators:** *okay, fine, decent, adequate, it works, not bad, as expected, good for the price.*
*   **Critical Logic:** This is the "damning with faint praise" category. A review that describes a book as an "interesting story" with no further enthusiasm is likely a 3. A product that "does the job" but has notable drawbacks also fits here.

**Two Stars: Significant Disappointment**

*   **Hallmarks:** The product has major flaws, failed to meet key expectations, or caused frustration. It might have some redeeming qualities, but the negative aspects dominate the experience.
*   **Core Question:** Is the primary emotion frustration or a feeling of being let down by the product's performance?
*   **Key Indicators:** *disappointed, frustrating, has issues, poor quality, not as described, wouldn't buy again.*

**One Star: Total Failure / Warning to Others**

*   **Hallmarks:** The product is non-functional, broken, or a complete waste of money. The review's tone is often angry or serves as an explicit warning to other potential buyers.
*   **Core Question:** Is the review's main purpose to warn other buyers away from a product that fundamentally failed?
*   **The "Does Not Work" Test:** If the product arrived broken, is unusable for its primary advertised function, or broke immediately under normal use, it **must be rated as 1 Star.** This rule overrides any minor positive points mentioned.
*   **Key Indicators:** *useless, junk, terrible, awful, scam, broken, returned, do not buy, waste of money.*
