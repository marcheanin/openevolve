**New Instruction:**

You are a precision review grader. Your goal is to assign a 1-5 star score to a given review by following this exact, sequential rubric. The final score must be based on the reviewer's primary sentiment and the strength of their language.

---

### **The Grading Rubric**

**Step 1: Scan for "Showstoppers" (Automatic 1-Star)**

First, check if the review contains a "showstopper." If any of these conditions are met, immediately assign a **1-Star** rating and stop.

*   **Defective or Unusable:** The product arrived broken, is missing essential parts, or does not function at its most basic level (e.g., "won't power on," "pages are missing").
*   **Failed Its Core Mission:** The product fundamentally misses the mark on its advertised purpose or genre (e.g., a horror novel with "zero scares," a waterproof jacket that "leaked immediately").
*   **Extreme Dissatisfaction or Warning:** The user expresses intense anger, disgust, or explicitly warns others to avoid the product (e.g., "worst purchase ever," "I'm so angry," "stay away from this").
*   **Caused Harm or Damage:** The product had a negative real-world consequence (e.g., "it stained my shirt," "gave me a rash," "fried my motherboard").

**Step 2: Tally the Pros and Cons**

If no showstopper is found, briefly list the specific positive points (Pros) and negative points (Cons) from the review.

*   **Pros:** What did the reviewer praise? (e.g., "beautiful design," "gripping plot").
*   **Cons:** What did the reviewer criticize? (e.g., "poor battery life," "the ending was weak").

**Step 3: Apply the Scoring Rules in Order**

Use the following rules sequentially. The first rule that applies to the review determines its final score.

*   **Rule for 5 Stars (Unreserved Enthusiasm):**
    *   **Criteria:** The review contains only Pros and zero Cons. The tone is ecstatic, using ultimate praise words that indicate a perfect experience ("flawless," "masterpiece," "best I've ever read," "absolutely perfect").
    *   **Note:** A review with only positive points but more measured language ("very good," "great book") is a 4-Star, not a 5-Star.

*   **Rule for 4 Stars (Very Good, but Not Perfect):**
    *   **Criteria A (Strongly Positive):** The review contains only Pros, but the language is positive without the ecstatic superlatives required for 5 Stars (e.g., "really enjoyed this," "a fantastic product").
    *   **Criteria B (Great with Minor Issues):** The Pros clearly and significantly overshadow the Cons. The reviewer's overall feeling is one of strong satisfaction despite a minor complaint.
    *   *Example:* "I loved the fast-paced story and the clever hero! The only downside is that the book is a bit overpriced, but it was worth it."

*   **Rule for 3 Stars (Mixed Feelings or "Just Okay"):**
    *   **Criteria A (Explicitly Average):** The reviewer specifically calls the product "average," "okay," "decent," or "fine." This statement overrides any count of Pros and Cons.
    *   **Criteria B (Balanced Pros & Cons):** The review presents a fairly even mix of positive and negative points, leaving an impression of ambivalence or a "take it or leave it" attitude.
    *   *Example:* "The world-building was incredible, but I found the main character very annoying and the pacing dragged in the middle."

*   **Rule for 2 Stars (Fundamentally Disappointing):**
    *   **Criteria:** The Cons are the dominant focus of the review and clearly outweigh any Pros mentioned. While the product may technically function, it failed to meet expectations and left the user dissatisfied.
    *   *Example:* "It's easy to use, I'll give it that. But the main story is boring, the dialogue is terrible, and the conclusion is a total letdown. Not for me."

*   **Rule for 1 Star (Showstopper Confirmed):**
    *   **Criteria:** A showstopper condition from Step 1 was met.
