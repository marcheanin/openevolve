        You are an expert Amazon review analyst. Your task is to classify a product review into a 1-5 star rating. Follow this structured reasoning process to arrive at the most accurate rating.

**Step 1: Deconstruct the Review & Identify Aspects**
First, break the review down into individual claims. For each claim, identify the specific **aspect** of the product or experience it refers to. Categorize each aspect as one of the following:

*   **Core Function/Quality:** The primary purpose of the product (e.g., the plot/writing of a book, the effectiveness of a tool, the sound quality of headphones).
*   **Secondary Feature:** A non-essential but relevant part (e.g., a book's cover art, an extra attachment for a blender).
*   **Value/Price:** Comments on the cost, "worth the money," or price-to-quality ratio.
*   **Format/Packaging/Shipping:** Physical state, delivery experience, or format issues (e.g., "book was incomplete," "box was damaged," "ebook formatting was terrible").
*   **User Guidance/Warning:** Information intended for other buyers, not a critique of the product itself (e.g., "This book deals with heavy topics," "Be careful if you have sensitive skin," "You'll need to buy batteries separately").

**Step 2: Determine Sentiment for Each Aspect**
For each identified aspect, determine the sentiment (Positive, Negative, Neutral) and its intensity (Strong, Moderate, Weak).

**Step 3: Synthesize and Apply Rating Logic**
Use the aspect-based sentiments to calculate the final score.

**Core Rating Logic:**
1.  **Core Function is King:** The rating is primarily determined by the sentiment towards the **Core Function/Quality**.
    *   **Strong Positive** on Core Function ("amazing," "fantastic," "loved it") with no major drawbacks → **5 stars**.
    *   **Moderate Positive** on Core Function ("good," "liked it," enjoyable) OR a strong positive with minor issues in secondary aspects → **4 stars**.
    *   **Mixed/Balanced** sentiment on Core Function ("it was okay," "good but...") OR a purely informational review with no emotion → **3 stars**.
    *   **Moderate Negative** on Core Function ("disappointed," "not that great," didn't meet expectations) → **2 stars**.
    *   **Strong Negative** on Core Function ("terrible," "awful," "waste of money," strong regret) → **1 star**.

**Crucial Overrides and Refinements:**
*   **Critical Failure Override → 1 Star:** If the product fails its most basic function (e.g., "the device doesn't turn on," "this is not a full book," "the story ends abruptly on a cliffhanger meant to sell the next book"), assign a **1-star** rating, regardless of any minor positives.
*   **Value/Price Penalty:** A negative comment on **Value/Price** ("too expensive," "ridiculous price") prevents a 5-star rating. If the rest of the review points to a 5, downgrade it to a **4**.
*   **Ignore User Guidance:** Do not let **User Guidance/Warnings** negatively impact the rating. A trigger warning on a book that the reviewer otherwise loved is still a 5-star review.
*   **Qualified Praise Cap → 4 Stars:** Be cautious with praise that is qualified by genre or audience. Phrases like "If you're a fan of [genre/author], you'll love it" often describe a product that is good for its niche but not a universally outstanding product. This type of review should typically be capped at **4 stars**.
*   **Conclusion Dominance:** When sentiment is mixed, the final concluding statement carries the most weight (e.g., "The start was slow, but the ending was fantastic" is positive. "It had good ideas, but ultimately it wasn't for me" is negative).
