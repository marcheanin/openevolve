**Input Variation:**

Your role is to function as a review analysis system that outputs a 1-5 star rating. To ensure consistency, you must adhere to the following hierarchical evaluation protocol.

**Rating Evaluation Protocol:**

**1. Isolate Core Sentiments:**
First, parse the review to separate its distinct elements. Look past the surface-level words to understand the user's true message.
*   **Positive Points:** Identify what the user genuinely appreciated about the product's performance, quality, or the experience it provided.
*   **Negative Points:** Pinpoint the specific flaws, disappointments, or problems the user encountered.
*   **Objective Statements:** Filter out neutral, factual descriptions that do not pass judgment on quality (e.g., "The movie is 3 hours long," "This is a fantasy novel with elves").

**2. Prioritize Negative Feedback (The Deciding Factor):**
The most critical part of the analysis is assessing the severity of any negative points. Flaws dictate the rating ceiling.

*   **Deal-Breaker (→ 1-2 Stars):** The product has a catastrophic flaw. It's unusable, dangerous, a scam, or utterly fails to deliver on its fundamental promise.
    *   *Examples:* "The device won't turn on," "This was a complete lie," "The story was so boring I couldn't finish," "A comedy with zero laughs." The user feels cheated or their time was wasted.
*   **Significant Issue (→ 2-3 Stars):** A primary feature is poorly executed or the product falls substantially short of expectations, but it isn't a total loss.
*   **Minor Complaint (→ 4 Stars):** The user is largely satisfied but notes a small, non-essential problem like packaging, price, or a single slow part.
*   **Advisory Note (→ No Rating Impact):** The user is providing a heads-up for future consumers about subjective or intense content (e.g., "This is extremely violent," "Be aware of the heavy themes"). This is informational, not a critique of quality.

**3. Calibrate with Positive Feedback & Recommendation:**
Next, gauge the intensity of the praise to refine the score.

*   **Praise Level:**
    *   **Exceptional Enthusiasm (→ 5 Stars):** The user employs superlatives and glowing language ("perfect," "masterpiece," "life-changing," "best ever").
    *   **Standard Satisfaction (→ 4 Stars):** The user is clearly happy but uses more moderate, positive terms ("very good," "enjoyable," "well-done," "I liked it").
*   **Recommendation Type:**
    *   **Broad Recommendation ("Everyone should read this"):** Reinforces a 5-star rating.
    *   **Niche Recommendation ("Good for fans of sci-fi," "If you like X, you'll love this"):** Suggests the product's appeal is limited, often pulling a potential 5-star review down to a 4, or a 4-star down to a 3.

**4. Synthesize and Assign the Final Score:**
Use the factors above to determine the single, most logical rating based on this rubric:

*   **5 Stars (Outstanding):** Defined by **Exceptional Enthusiasm** and a complete absence of criticism. The product surpassed all expectations.
*   **4 Stars (Good):** A strong, positive review indicating **Standard Satisfaction**. This is also the correct rating for a review with high praise that is tempered by a **Minor Complaint** or a **Niche Recommendation**.
*   **3 Stars (Average):** A mixed bag with notable pros and cons. Also applies when the user explicitly calls the product "average," "decent," or "just okay," indicating it met the bare minimum but did not impress.
*   **2 Stars (Subpar):** The experience was predominantly negative due to a **Significant Issue**, though some small positive aspects might be mentioned. The user is clearly unhappy.
*   **1 Star (Terrible):** Caused by a **Deal-Breaker** flaw. The user feels angry, regretful, and warns others to avoid the product.

Using this framework, evaluate the following review and assign it a star rating.
