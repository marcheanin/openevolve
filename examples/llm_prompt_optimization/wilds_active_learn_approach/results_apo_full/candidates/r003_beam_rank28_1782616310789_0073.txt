**New Instruction:**

Your role is to function as an expert review analyst. Your objective is to accurately determine a 1-to-5 star rating for a product review. Use the following framework to guide your decision, focusing on identifying the most impactful elements of the user's feedback.

**A Framework for Rating Reviews:**

**Guiding Principle:** First, separate subjective feedback (praise/criticism) from objective descriptions. A statement like "This book is about war" or "This product requires assembly" is neutral information for other buyers and should not influence the star rating.

**1. The Deal-Breaker Test (Identifying 1-Star Reviews)**

Start by looking for a critical, non-negotiable flaw. If one exists, the rating is almost always 1 star.
*   **Definition:** A critical flaw means the product is fundamentally unusable, defective, dangerous, or fraudulent. It completely fails to serve its primary function.
*   **Clues:** Look for phrases like "arrived broken," "stopped working after one week," "scam," "false advertising," or "completely unusable." The user's emotion is typically anger, frustration, or a desire to warn others.

**2. The Ecstatic User Test (Identifying 5-Star Reviews)**

If there are no deal-breakers, check for overwhelming, enthusiastic praise. This is the hallmark of a 5-star experience.
*   **Definition:** The user is thrilled, not just satisfied. Their praise is specific, emotional, and often uses superlatives.
*   **Clues:** Look for words like "perfect," "masterpiece," "best ever," "amazing," "loved it," or strong emotional reactions like "I couldn't put it down." Any "negatives" are often framed as positives (e.g., "The only downside is that I finished it too quickly!").

**3. The Middle-Ground Analysis (Distinguishing 2, 3, and 4 Stars)**

If the review is neither a 1-star disaster nor a 5-star rave, it falls into the 2-4 star range. The final rating depends on the balance between its pros and cons.

*   **To assign a 4-Star Rating (Good):** The review is solidly positive.
    *   It contains **standard praise** ("good," "fun," "works as expected," "enjoyable").
    *   OR, it has strong praise but is tempered by a **minor issue** that doesn't affect the core function (e.g., "The packaging was a bit flimsy," "The plot was a little slow in the middle").
    *   OR, it's a **qualified but enthusiastic recommendation** for a specific group ("A must-have for sci-fi fans").

*   **To assign a 3-Star Rating (Average/Mixed):** The review reflects a mixed or mediocre experience.
    *   It contains a **significant negative** that detracts from a core feature, balanced by some positive aspects.
    *   OR, the overall feeling is lukewarm and unenthusiastic ("It was just okay," "Nothing special").
    *   OR, the praise is **heavily qualified** and suggests the product is only suitable for a very niche audience.

*   **To assign a 2-Star Rating (Poor):** The review is dominated by negativity.
    *   A **major disappointment** is the central theme. A core aspect of the product was deeply flawed or failed to meet a primary expectation, even if it wasn't completely broken.
    *   There may be a minor positive mentioned, but it's overshadowed by the significant flaws.

**Final Synthesis:**

Based on the tests above, assign the single most appropriate star rating. A 1-star is for a critical failure, a 5-star is for an exceptional experience, and 2-4 stars represent the spectrum of good, mixed, and poor outcomes in between. Now, apply this framework to the user's review to determine its star rating.
