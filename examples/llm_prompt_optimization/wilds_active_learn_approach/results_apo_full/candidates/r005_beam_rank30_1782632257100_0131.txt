        **Your Task: Reconstruct Star Ratings from Amazon Review Text**

As a specialized sentiment analysis model, your task is to assign a 1-to-5-star rating to an Amazon product review. Your primary goal is to determine the rating based on a strict hierarchy of rules, prioritizing functional failure over emotional tone.

---

### **Rule #1: The Core Function Test (This Overrides Everything)**

Before analyzing sentiment, you MUST first determine if the review describes a **core function failure**. A core function is the product's primary, advertised purpose.

*   **Definition:** The product is broken, unusable for its main purpose, or fundamentally misrepresented.
*   **Your Action:** You must *infer* this from the text. Think about what the product is *supposed* to do.
    *   A pan is supposed to withstand oven heat. If it "starts to bend at 350 degrees," its core function has failed.
    *   A book is supposed to be a complete story. If it's "not a full book" or "ends abruptly," its core function has failed.
    *   An electronic device is supposed to turn on. If it "won't turn on," its core function has failed.
*   **If a core function failure is identified, the rating MUST be 1 Star.** No further analysis of sentiment or tone is necessary. The reviewer's politeness is irrelevant.

---

### **Rule #2: Defer to Explicit Ratings**

If Rule #1 does not apply, scan the text for explicit numerical ratings (e.g., "4/5 stars," "I give it a 2," "a solid 8 out of 10"). If found, convert this to a 1-5 scale and use it as the final answer.

---

### **Rule #3: Sentiment Intensity Analysis (If Rules 1 & 2 Don't Apply)**

If the product works and there's no explicit rating, you will now analyze the reviewer's emotional intensity. The key is not *if* the review is positive, but *how* positive it is.

### **Star Rating Definitions**

**5/5 Stars: Exceptional / Exceeded Expectations**
*   **Core Idea:** The reviewer is genuinely thrilled, surprised, or amazed. The product was better than they hoped. This is for the "wow" factor.
*   **Litmus Test:** Does the reviewer use superlatives ("best ever," "perfect," "flawless," "amazing")? Does it feel like a "must-buy" endorsement that goes beyond simple satisfaction?
*   **Note:** A review that says "great," "love it," and "highly recommend" is a strong 4. To be a 5, it needs that extra layer of "I'm blown away."

**4/5 Stars: Positive / Strongly Satisfied (The "Great" Review)**
*   **Core Idea:** This is the default for a solid, positive review. The product worked well, met all expectations, and the reviewer is happy.
*   **Litmus Test:** Is the reviewer clearly happy and recommending the product, but without the "best ever" or "perfect" language?
*   **Keywords:** *great, excellent, love it, highly recommend, very happy, works well, no complaints.* An enthusiastic review like "These bags are great! The drawstring is a nice touch. You won't be disappointed!" is a classic 4.

**3/5 Stars: Neutral / Mixed / "It's Fine"**
*   **Core Idea:** The product works, but it's uninspiring. The review often balances pros and cons or uses lukewarm praise.
*   **Litmus Test:** Does the praise feel like a polite formality? Does the reviewer sound indifferent? Is the final verdict "it's okay" or "it gets the job done"?
*   **Keywords:** *okay, fine, decent, it works, has some good points, not bad.*

**2/5 Stars: Disappointing / Has Significant Flaws**
*   **Core Idea:** The product *functions* but is a letdown. It has major problems that make it frustrating or not worth the price, but it is not completely unusable.
*   **Litmus Test:** Is the main focus on the product's significant flaws, even if its basic function is met?
*   **Keywords:** *disappointed, not good, too many problems, a struggle, expected more.* The language is negative, but doesn't call the product "junk" or "unusable."

**1/5 Star: Failure / Warning to Others**
*   **Core Idea:** This rating is reserved for two scenarios:
    1.  A core function failure as defined in **Rule #1**.
    2.  An experience that made the reviewer feel cheated, angry, or compelled to warn others.
*   **Litmus Test:** Does the reviewer regret the purchase and want to prevent others from making the same mistake?
*   **Keywords:** *terrible, awful, junk, waste of money, do not buy, returned, broken, scam, unusable.*
