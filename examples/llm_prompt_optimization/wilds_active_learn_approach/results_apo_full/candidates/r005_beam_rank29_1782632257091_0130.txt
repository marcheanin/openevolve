**Your Task: Reconstruct Star Ratings from Amazon Review Text**

As a sentiment analysis model, your objective is to assign a 1-to-5-star rating to a given product review. Your analysis must precisely capture the reviewer's overall satisfaction and enthusiasm, considering the specific nuances of their language.

### **Rule #1: Defer to Explicit Ratings**

This is the most critical rule and overrides all other analysis.

Before analyzing sentiment, you MUST scan the text for any explicit numerical rating.
-   Examples: "5/5", "I rate this 4 stars", "a solid 8 out of 10", "one star".
-   If found, this number is the definitive rating. Convert it to a 1-5 scale (e.g., 8/10 becomes 4 stars) and output it as the final answer. No further analysis is needed.

### **Rule #2: Sentiment Analysis Protocol (If No Explicit Rating)**

If and only if no explicit rating is found, proceed with the following steps.

**Core Principles:**

1.  **Measure Intensity:** The primary differentiator is the *intensity* and *quality* of the emotion, not just the presence of positive or negative words.
2.  **Consider the Frame of Reference:** Pay attention to phrases like "for the price," "for a budget option," or "for a beginner." A product can be rated 5 stars if it's considered perfect *within its stated category*, even if the reviewer acknowledges it's not the best product overall.
3.  **Identify the Pivot:** Locate contrastive conjunctions like "but," "however," or "although." The sentiment expressed *after* such a word typically carries more weight and dictates the final rating.

**Analytical Steps:**
1.  Read the entire review to establish the frame of reference and overall tone.
2.  Apply the refined rating definitions below to determine the final star rating.

---

### **Refined Star Rating Definitions**

**5/5 Stars: Exceptional / New Favorite / "Perfect for its Purpose"**
-   **Description:** The product is described as flawless, a new benchmark, or a personal favorite. It perfectly met or vastly exceeded all expectations with no significant reservations.
-   **Test:** Does the reviewer imply this is the **best they've used** or **perfect for its specific purpose/price point**? Is the praise unqualified and definitive?
-   **Keywords:** *perfect, flawless, best ever, life-changing, mind-blowing, holy grail, can't live without, new favorite*.
-   **Crucial Limiter:** A review is **NOT 5 stars** if it contains a significant caveat or warning (e.g., "but the ending is a cliffhanger," "the battery life is just okay"). A strong recommendation like "must-read" or "must-buy" is capped at 4 stars if it is qualified in any way (e.g., "for fans of the genre," or contains a warning).

**4/5 Stars: Excellent / Strong Recommendation with Minor Caveats**
-   **Description:** This is the default for a very strong, positive review. The user is happy, the product works great, and they recommend it. It's a strong endorsement that falls just short of "perfection" or has minor qualifications.
-   **Test:** Is the reviewer enthusiastic and happy, but mentions a minor flaw, a warning, or acknowledges a better alternative exists?
-   **Keywords:** *excellent, great, fantastic, love it, highly recommend, very happy, almost perfect*.
-   **Key Use Case:** This is the correct rating for reviews that are overwhelmingly positive but contain a qualifier. For example: "A must-read for mafia romance fans, but be warned, it ends on a cliffhanger." Or, "An extremely comfortable mouse, though I prefer the more expensive M705 for its extra buttons."

**3/5 Stars: Mixed / Adequate / "It's Fine"**
-   **Description:** The product is functional but uninspiring, or the review has a significant balance of pros and cons. The overall feeling is lukewarm or one of mild disappointment relative to expectations.
-   **Test:** Does the review feel like a verbal shrug? Does it say "it works, but..."?
-   **Keywords:** *okay, fine, decent, it works, average, not bad, has pros and cons*.
-   **Handling Disappointment:** If a reviewer is a fan of a series/brand and finds a new entry "not as good" or a "let down" but doesn't call the product itself a failure, this is often a 3. The final sentiment might be negative ("thumbs down"), but the context is disappointment, not anger.

**2/5 Stars: Disappointing / Significant Flaws**
-   **Description:** The product has major issues that prevent it from working as intended, or it failed to meet basic expectations. The negative aspects are the dominant theme of the review, and the tone is one of frustration.
-   **Test:** Is the reviewer's main goal to highlight serious problems that make the product difficult or unpleasant to use?
-   **Keywords:** *disappointed, poor quality, doesn't work well, too many problems, wouldn't buy again, frustrating*.

**1/5 Star: Failure / Warning to Others**
-   **Description:** A complete failure of the product or an experience that made the reviewer angry or feel cheated. The primary purpose of the review is to warn other potential buyers away.
-   **Test:** Does the reviewer regret their purchase and want to prevent others from making the same mistake?
-   **Keywords:** *terrible, awful, junk, waste of money, do not buy, returned, broken, unusable*.

---

### **Critical Failure Clause**

-   If a review states that a product's core function failed (e.g., "arrived broken," "won't turn on," "book is missing chapters"), it MUST be rated as **1 Star**, even if the reviewer mentions minor positive aspects.
