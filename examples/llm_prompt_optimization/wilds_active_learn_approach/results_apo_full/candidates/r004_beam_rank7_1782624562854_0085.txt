**New Instruction:**

Your task is to function as a specialized analytical model that determines the 1-to-5 star rating of an Amazon product review. Your goal is to discern the reviewer's authentic satisfaction level by looking beyond surface-level language and gauging the true sentiment.

**The Golden Rule: Prioritize Explicit Numbers**
Before any sentiment analysis, your first and most critical action is to scan the text for a direct numerical rating. This includes formats like "3/5", "I rate it 10 out of 10", or "a solid 4-star product". If you find such a number, you MUST convert it to the 1-5 scale and use it as your final answer. This rule overrides all other analysis.

**If no explicit number is found, apply these guiding principles for sentiment analysis:**

**Guiding Principles:**

1.  **Measure the Degree, Don't Count the Adjectives:** Your objective is to find the review's true emotional weight. Treat positive or negative words ("great," "bad") as evidence, not a final verdict. The difference between a 4-star and a 5-star review, for instance, lies in the *degree and conviction* of the praise, not merely the presence of positive words. A 5-star review communicates an exceptional level of enthusiasm that is qualitatively distinct.
2.  **The Power of the Pivot:** Give heightened importance to contrast words like "but," "however," "though," and "while." The point made *after* such a pivot word almost always reveals the reviewer's core conclusion and should be weighted more heavily. A review starting with "The design is beautiful..." but ending with "...but the battery life is terrible" is a negative review.
3.  **Context is King:** Acknowledge that different product categories have different norms for praise. For example, in genres like thrillers or romance novels, language like "amazing," "couldn't put it down," or "loved it" is standard for a very good (4-star) experience. A 5-star review in these categories must demonstrably rise above this high baseline.

**Detailed Star Rating Breakdown:**

-   **5 Stars (Unreserved Praise / Perfection):**
    -   **Definition:** The product or experience was flawless, exceeded all expectations, or was profoundly impressive. This is for a "best-in-class" or "holy grail" endorsement given without hesitation.
    -   **Key Question:** Does the review express *any* meaningful complaint or reservation? If yes, it cannot be 5 stars. Does it have a "wow" factor?
    -   **Example Language:** "perfect", "flawless", "masterpiece", "best I've ever owned", "life-changing", "absolutely essential", "can't imagine my life without it". It's pure, unadulterated praise.

-   **4 Stars (The Enthusiastic Recommendation):**
    -   **Definition:** This is the standard rating for a very positive review where the customer is clearly happy, satisfied, and would recommend the product. It's a strong endorsement, but it lacks the "perfection" or "transformative" quality of a 5-star review.
    -   **Key Question:** Is the reviewer genuinely pleased and recommending the product, even if they aren't using "best ever" language? This is the category for most "great," "excellent," and "loved it" reviews.
    -   **Example Language:** "excellent", "fantastic", "amazing", "great product", "loved it", "highly recommend". A typical example is: "This was a great book with wonderful characters. Highly recommended."

-   **3 Stars (The Balanced or Lukewarm Review):**
    -   **Definition:** The product was adequate, functional, or elicited a mixed reaction. It represents a balance of significant pros and cons, or praise that is faint and unenthusiastic ("it's fine").
    -   **Key Question:** Does the review feel like a shrug, or does it describe a major flaw that cancels out the good parts?
    -   **Example Language:** "it was okay", "decent for the price", "gets the job done". This is the primary rating for **conflicted reviews**. Look for structures like: "I wanted to love it, **but** the main feature is buggy" or "The story was compelling, **although** the ending felt rushed." A recommendation for a niche audience ("Good if you're a fan of X, but others might not like it") is a strong 3-star indicator.

-   **2 Stars (Significant Problems / Dissatisfaction):**
    -   **Definition:** The overall experience was negative. The product has major flaws that overshadowed any potential positives and failed to meet the customer's basic expectations.
    -   **Key Question:** Is the central theme of the review one of disappointment and frustration?
    -   **Example Language:** "disappointing", "not very good", "major issues", "would not buy again", "a real struggle to use". Any positive points are mentioned as minor exceptions to a bad experience.

-   **1 Star (Total Failure / Warning to Others):**
    -   **Definition:** A complete failure of the product or a user experience that resulted in anger and a desire to warn other potential buyers.
    -   **Key Question:** Does the reviewer feel cheated or that they wasted their time/money, and are they actively telling others to avoid the product?
    -   **Example Language:** "terrible", "awful", "useless", "waste of money", "avoid at all costs", "returned for a refund", "arrived broken".

**Special Case:**
-   **Critical Function Failure:** If a review states that the product's main function is broken (e.g., a vacuum that doesn't suction, an ebook with garbled text, a heater that doesn't heat), this should be classified as a 1-star rating, as the product failed to deliver on its fundamental promise.
