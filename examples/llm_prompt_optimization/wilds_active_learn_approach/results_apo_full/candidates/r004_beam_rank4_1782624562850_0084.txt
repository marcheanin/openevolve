### **Rating Protocol**

**Step 1: Triage for Deal-Breakers (Immediate 1-Star)**
Your first and most important check is for a "deal-breaker"—a fatal flaw that makes the product or experience unacceptable. If the review contains any of the following, you must assign a **1-Star** rating immediately and terminate the analysis.

*   **Critical Product Failure:** The item is defective, arrived broken, is non-functional, or cannot perform its core advertised purpose, rendering it useless. This is key: if a product fails its main job (e.g., a "waterproof" watch that leaks in the shower), it is a 1-star, even if it has other minor working features (e.g., the watch band is comfortable).
    *   **Examples:** "the device won't power on," "arrived with a crack," a mystery novel with "no suspense," a space heater that "barely gets warm."
*   **Fundamental Misrepresentation:** The product or experience is so different from its description that it causes deep frustration or a sense of being misled. This includes cases where the user gives up on the product.
    *   **Examples:** "this is not what was advertised," "I couldn't even finish it," "threw it out after trying to assemble it for an hour."
*   **Intense Negative Sentiment:** The review expresses extreme anger, disgust, or regret, and often includes a direct warning against purchasing. The overall feeling is one of total dissatisfaction.
    *   **Examples:** "an absolute waste of time and money," "I despise this product," "avoid at all costs," "deeply disappointing."
*   **Harmful Result:** The product caused a negative consequence (e.g., "it corrupted my files," "caused an allergic reaction").

**Step 2: Deconstruct the Review**
If no deal-breakers are found in Step 1, proceed to analyze the review's content. Create a simple breakdown by listing all specific positive feedback (Strengths) and negative feedback (Flaws).

*   **Strengths:** Enumerate the specific elements the reviewer liked (e.g., "fast-paced plot," "beautiful design").
*   **Flaws:** Enumerate all drawbacks, criticisms, or reservations mentioned (e.g., "the ending felt rushed," "overpriced," "has a major cliffhanger").

**Step 3: The Rating Cascade (Apply Rules in Strict Order)**
Use the following rules sequentially. The first rule that matches the review determines its star rating. Do not skip ahead.

*   **Rule 1: Check for 4 Stars (Very Good, but Not Perfect)**
    *   **Condition:** Before considering a 5-star rating, you must first screen for imperfections or qualifiers. If **ANY** of the following is true, assign **4 Stars** and conclude the rating.
        *   A) **Minor Flaw Mentioned:** The review is overwhelmingly positive but points out a non-critical Flaw. (e.g., "A brilliant thriller... My only complaint is the price is a bit high.")
        *   B) **Praise is Tempered:** The review includes a specific warning or caveat that slightly reduces the endorsement. (e.g., "Great book, but be warned, it ends on a cliffhanger.")
        *   C) **Praise is Vague:** The compliments are polite and positive but lack specific, enthusiastic detail. (e.g., "This was a well-written book that I enjoyed.")
        *   D) **Reviewer Disclosure:** The reviewer states they received a free or advanced copy (ARC). This automatically caps the rating at 4 stars. (e.g., "I received a complimentary copy for my honest review.")

*   **Rule 2: Check for 5 Stars (Flawless Enthusiasm)**
    *   **Condition:** **Only if** the review did not meet any 4-Star criteria, check for perfection. The review must contain specific, enthusiastic **Strengths** with **zero** identifiable **Flaws**, caveats, or disclosures. The tone is glowing and unreserved.
    *   **Example:** "Absolutely incredible story! The character development was masterful, and the suspense kept me on the edge of my seat. I couldn't put it down!"

*   **Rule 3: Check for 3 Stars (Mixed or Average)**
    *   **Condition:** The review expresses ambivalence or a balanced opinion.
        *   **Balanced Feedback:** Contains a clear mix of both **Strengths** and **Flaws**, where neither strongly outweighs the other.
        *   **Explicitly Average:** The reviewer uses language that directly calls the product adequate but unremarkable (e.g., "it was fine," "decent, but not amazing").
        *   **Major Caveat:** The recommendation is lukewarm or limited to a specific audience (e.g., "A good read for die-hard fans of the genre, but others may find it slow.").
    *   **Example:** "The plot was interesting, but the main character was very unlikable, which made it hard to get into."

*   **Rule 4: Check for 2 Stars (Mostly Negative)**
    *   **Condition:** The review has already passed the 1-Star "deal-breaker" check, but the **Flaws significantly overshadow** any mentioned **Strengths**. The user is clearly disappointed and would not recommend the product, even if it technically "works."
    *   **Example:** "While the camera quality is okay, the battery life is terrible and the software is full of bugs. I regret buying it."

*   **Rule 5: Confirm 1 Star**
    *   **Condition:** This rating is assigned if a deal-breaker was identified in Step 1.
