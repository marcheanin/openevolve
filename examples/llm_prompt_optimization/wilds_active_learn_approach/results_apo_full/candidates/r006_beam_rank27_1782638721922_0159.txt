### **Objective: Reconstruct the Star Rating from a Review**

Your goal is to accurately determine the 1-to-5 star rating a user assigned to a product based on the text of their review. This is a two-step process. Follow these rules in the specified order.

---

### **Step 1: The Literal Search (Highest Priority)**

Before any analysis, your first and only initial action is to scan the text for a literal, explicitly stated rating.

*   **Action:** Read the review and look for numbers or number-words that clearly indicate a score.
*   **Examples:** "I give this 5 stars", "a 2/5 experience", "rated it a solid four", "8 out of 10", "one-star product".
*   **The Golden Rule:** If you find an explicit rating, convert it to a 1-5 scale (e.g., 8/10 = 4 stars) and provide that number as the answer. **Do not proceed to Step 2.** Your work is done.

---

### **Step 2: The Inferential Analysis (Use Only If Necessary)**

If and only if the review contains no explicit score, you must infer the rating by analyzing the sentiment, tone, and overall message.

**Guiding Principles for Analysis:**

*   **Conclusion over Keywords:** The final rating should reflect the reviewer's overall conclusion, not just a count of positive or negative words. A review listing many good features but ending with "I'm still not sure I'd recommend it" is not a 4 or 5-star review.
*   **The "But" Factor:** Pay close attention to contrastive conjunctions ("but," "however," "although"). These words often signal a ceiling for the rating. Praise that comes before a "but" is almost always discounted by what follows.
*   **Balance of Evidence:** Mentally list the pros and cons. The final rating is a reflection of how the reviewer weighed them.

### **Rating Inference Guide (1-5 Stars)**

**5 Stars: Enthusiastic Endorsement**
*   **Core Idea:** The user is thrilled. The product surpassed their expectations and has become a new favorite. There is a sense of excitement or deep satisfaction. This is reserved for "wow" moments.
*   **Indicators:** Superlative language (*best, perfect, amazing, flawless, life-changing*). The review expresses an emotional, overwhelmingly positive response.
*   **Common Pitfall:** Do not award 5 stars for reviews that are merely positive. A simple "It's a great product, works well" is a 4-star review. A 5-star review needs that extra layer of enthusiasm.

**4 Stars: Solid Recommendation**
*   **Core Idea:** This is the default "good review." The product works as advertised, the user is happy, and they would recommend it. It met expectations without necessarily blowing them away.
*   **Indicators:** Positive but measured language (*great, excellent, very good, works perfectly, highly recommend*). The review confirms the product's quality and function without any significant complaints.
*   **Distinction:** A 4-star review can still acknowledge a better product exists ("This isn't as powerful as the pro model, but it's perfect for my needs"). The key is that the product *being reviewed* has no stated flaws.

**3 Stars: The "It's Fine" Review**
*   **Core Idea:** The product is adequate but not great. The reviewer's feelings are mixed, or the praise is faint. There is a significant trade-off.
*   **Indicators:** Lukewarm words (*okay, fine, decent, adequate, does the job*). The review presents a clear balance of pros and cons ("The screen is great, but the battery life is terrible").
*   **Logic:** This rating applies to "damning with faint praise" ("A decent effort") or when the overall conclusion is "meh" despite some positive points.

**2 Stars: Significant Flaws / Disappointment**
*   **Core Idea:** The product failed to meet basic expectations or has major problems that prevent normal enjoyment or use. The tone is one of frustration and dissatisfaction.
*   **Indicators:** Focus on what's wrong. Phrases like *disappointed, frustrating, has issues, not as described, wouldn't buy again*. The reviewer feels let down.
*   **Distinction:** Unlike a 3-star review with a pro/con balance, the negative aspects clearly outweigh any positives.

**1 Star: Total Failure or Public Warning**
*   **Core Idea:** The product is fundamentally broken, unusable, or the experience was so bad it serves as a warning to others. The tone is often angry or feels like a betrayal.
*   **Triggers:**
    *   **Complete Malfunction:** "Arrived broken," "doesn't work at all," "waste of money."
    *   **Misrepresentation:** The product was not what was advertised at all, leading to anger.
    *   **Strong Warning:** The reviewer's primary goal is to tell others "Do not buy this."

**Final Output:** A single number from 1 to 5.
