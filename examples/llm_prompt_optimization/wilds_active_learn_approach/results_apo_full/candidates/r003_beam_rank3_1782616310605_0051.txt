**Evaluation Framework:**

**Phase 1: Screen for Showstoppers (Automatic 1-Star)**
Begin by checking if the review highlights a "showstopper"—a critical flaw that renders the product or experience unacceptable. If any of the following criteria are met, assign a **1-Star** rating immediately and conclude the evaluation.

*   **Fundamental Malfunction or Misrepresentation:**
    *   **Product Failure:** The product is non-functional, arrived damaged, is missing parts, or its use was abandoned due to sheer frustration (e.g., "doesn't turn on," "arrived broken," "gave up trying to assemble it," **"I did not finish this book"**).
    *   **Experience Failure:** The product fundamentally misrepresents its purpose or fails to deliver on its core promise, causing profound disappointment (e.g., a story that makes the reader **"feel cheated,"** a thriller with "no tension," a heater that is only "lukewarm").
*   **Overwhelmingly Negative Emotion:** The user expresses intense anger, disgust, or regret, often with an explicit warning to others. The sentiment is one of complete dissatisfaction (e.g., "complete waste of money," "I hate that!", "do not buy this," **"very unsatisfying,"** "a cop out").
*   **Detrimental Outcome:** The product directly caused harm, whether physical or technical (e.g., "crashed my computer," "made my skin break out").

**Phase 2: Catalogue Strengths and Weaknesses**
If no showstopper is found, proceed to break down the review into its core components. List all specific positive points (Strengths) and negative points (Weaknesses).

*   **Strengths:** Note the specific aspects the user enjoyed or praised (e.g., "great photography," "suspenseful story," "easy to assemble").
*   **Weaknesses:** Note the specific drawbacks, flaws, or reservations mentioned, even if minor (e.g., "abrupt ending," "price is a little ridiculous," **"no masterpiece"**).

**Phase 3: Apply the Scoring Rubric (Sequential Rules)**
Use the following rules in the exact order they are presented. The first rule that accurately describes the review determines the final star rating.

*   **Rule for 5 Stars (Unreservedly Positive):**
    *   **Condition:** The review offers specific, enthusiastic **Strengths** and has **zero** identifiable **Weaknesses**. The tone is glowing and suggests expectations were surpassed.
    *   **Example:** "This was a fantastic story! The way the author wove the subplot about the missing heirloom through the main narrative was genius. So much suspense. I loved it!"

*   **Rule for 4 Stars (Largely Positive with Minor Reservations):**
    *   **Condition:** The user is clearly satisfied and would recommend the product, but stops short of a perfect score. This is true if:
        *   A) **Dominant Strengths:** The review is filled with praise, but includes a minor, non-critical **Weakness**. The overall feeling is one of happiness. (e.g., "Another great novel... loaded with suspense! However, the prices are getting a little ridiculous.")
        *   B) **Generic Positivity:** The praise is general and lacks the specific, effusive detail of a 5-star review, or it contains a disclaimer. (e.g., "The author did an excellent job. I enjoyed reading this book.", "I thought this book was a great read... *I received this complimentary book...").

*   **Rule for 3 Stars (Balanced or Ambivalent):**
    *   **Condition:** The user has mixed feelings, or the recommendation is lukewarm. The review fits one of these patterns:
        *   **Balanced Feedback:** Contains a significant mix of both **Strengths** and **Weaknesses**, with neither side clearly dominating.
        *   **Explicitly "Average":** The user directly states the product is adequate but not special (e.g., "it was okay," **"no masterpiece, but serves as terrific entertainment,"** "good, but not great").
        *   **Qualified Recommendation:** The praise is tepid or comes with major caveats, often suggesting the product is only for a specific audience. (e.g., **"An interesting opportunity to see an early effort... well worth your time if you are a fan."**).
    *   **Example:** "Good story, but the ending was abrupt and felt rushed."

*   **Rule for 2 Stars (Predominantly Negative):**
    *   **Condition:** Having passed Phase 1, the **Weaknesses significantly overshadow** any **Strengths**. The product is functional but deeply flawed or disappointing, and the user would not recommend it.
    *   **Example:** "I managed to finish this, but the characters were underdeveloped and the plot wasn't gripping. While I might try this author again, this particular book just wasn't for me."

*   **Rule for 1 Star (Showstopper Confirmed):**
    *   **Condition:** A showstopper was identified during the initial screening in Phase 1.
    *   **Example:** "I enjoyed this very well written book until I got to the end. Then I felt cheated. Where was the conclusion? ... Very unsatisfying."
