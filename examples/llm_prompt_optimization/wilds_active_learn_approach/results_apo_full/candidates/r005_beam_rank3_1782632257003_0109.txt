**Step 1: Check for an Explicit Rating (Highest Priority)**
First, scan the text for any explicit rating like "4/5 stars," "I give it a three," or "9 out of 10." If found, convert it to a 1-5 scale and provide that number as your final answer. This rule overrides all other analysis.

**If no explicit rating is found, proceed to Step 2.**

**Step 2: Triage for Extremes (1-Star and 5-Star)**
Before analyzing nuanced sentiment, check if the review fits a clear 1-star or 5-star profile.

*   **1-Star Test (Catastrophic Failure):** Does the review describe a product that is fundamentally broken, unusable, arrived damaged, or completely failed to deliver on its core promise? This includes a book with missing pages, a device that won't turn on, or a product described with words like "scam," "garbage," "unusable," "returned it," or "waste of money." A story that is described as having "no tension" or being "lukewarm" when it's supposed to be "scorching hot" also falls into this category of failing its core promise. If it meets this test, classify it as **1**.

*   **5-Star Test (Flawless Enthusiasm):** This is a two-part test. The review must pass **both** parts to be a 5.
    1.  **Enthusiastic Praise:** Does the text express overwhelming, unreserved satisfaction? Look for phrases like "perfect," "flawless," "best ever," "could not put it down," "exceeded all expectations," "life-changing," or "a must-read." The overall tone must be ecstatic.
    2.  **The Flaw Check (Crucial):** After confirming enthusiastic praise, perform a final check for **ANY** reservations, flaws, or warnings. If a negative point is mentioned—even a minor one like "ends on a cliffhanger," "a bit pricey," or "the color is slightly off"—it is **immediately disqualified** from a 5-star rating and should be considered a 4.

    If a review has ecstatic praise AND zero mentioned flaws, classify it as **5**.

**If the review is not a clear 1 or 5, proceed to Step 3.**

**Step 3: Differentiate the Mid-Range (4, 3, 2 Stars)**

*   **4 Stars (The Strong "Yes"):** This is the default rating for a **very positive review**. The customer is happy, would recommend the product, and feels they got their money's worth, but isn't "ecstatic."

You won't be disappointed!" is a classic 4. A review that has ecstatic praise but mentions a flaw (like a cliffhanger) also belongs here.

*   **3 Stars (The Mixed "Meh..."):** This rating is for reviews that are either lukewarm or a mixed bag.
    *   **Signals:** Look for faint praise ("it was okay," "fine," "decent") or a clear conflict where significant positives are balanced by significant negatives ("I loved the characters, but the plot was a major problem"). The final verdict is "good, BUT..."

*   **2 Stars (The Disappointed "No"):** The overall experience was negative. The product had significant flaws that overshadowed any good parts and failed to meet basic expectations. The reviewer is clearly unhappy and would not recommend it.
    *   **Signals:** "disappointed," "not very good," "too many issues," "a struggle to get through." This is for products that *work* but are deeply flawed, unlike the non-functional products in the 1-star category.

**Final Instruction:** Based on this logic, output the single most appropriate integer rating.
