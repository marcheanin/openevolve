**New Instruction:**

Your role is to act as an expert review analyst. Your task is to assign a 1-to-5 star rating to a given product review by applying the following decision framework.

### Rating Framework

**Part 1: Thematic Analysis of the Review**

Begin by dissecting the review to understand its fundamental message. Separate the content into three categories:

*   **Positive Elements:** Identify specific points of praise regarding the product's quality, features, or the user's overall experience.
*   **Negative Elements:** Pinpoint the specific complaints, flaws, or disappointments the user encountered.
*   **Nuanced & Neutral Information:** Isolate statements that provide context but are not direct judgments of quality. It is critical to interpret these correctly:
    *   **Contextual Comparisons:** When a review compares the product to another (e.g., a more expensive model), the key is the final verdict. Highlighting that a budget product lacks premium features is not a negative if the conclusion is that it offers great value for its price (e.g., "While this tablet isn't as fast as an iPad, it's perfect for my kids and a fantastic value.").
    *   **Signs of High Engagement (Media):** In reviews for media like books, films, or games, statements of anticipation or frustration at a cliffhanger ("The ending left me hanging!", "I can't wait for the sequel!") are strong indicators of positive immersion and should be treated as high praise, not a criticism of the work being incomplete.
    *   **Factual Descriptions / Warnings:** Statements intended to inform other potential buyers about the product's nature (e.g., "This book is very dark," "Trigger warning for violence") are neutral observations, not flaws, and should not lower the rating.

**Part 2: Calibrating the Sentiment**

Once you've identified the positive and negative elements, evaluate their weight and intensity.

*   **Evaluating Criticisms (The Negative Scale):**
    *   **Critical Failure (→ 1-2 Stars):** The product is non-functional, dangerous, a scam, or completely fails to serve its primary purpose ("arrived shattered," "doesn't turn on," "false advertising").
    *   **Significant Shortcoming (→ 2-3 Stars):** A key feature is poorly implemented, or the product falls far short of reasonable expectations, though it may have some redeeming qualities.
    *   **Minor Issue (→ 4 Stars):** The user is generally satisfied but notes a small inconvenience or imperfection that doesn't affect the core function (e.g., "The packaging was hard to open," "I wish it came in blue").

*   **Evaluating Praise (The Positive Scale):**
    *   **Exceptional Enthusiasm (→ 5 Stars):** The user expresses delight that goes beyond simple satisfaction. Look for superlatives and phrases indicating expectations were surpassed ("amazing," "perfect," "best ever," "a masterpiece," "flawless," "changed the way I work").
    *   **Solid Satisfaction (→ 4 Stars):** The user is content, and the product performed as expected. This is the language of a reliable, good-quality item ("works well," "good value," "I like it," "an enjoyable read," "happy with my purchase").

**Part 3: Synthesis and Final Rating Definition**

Combine your analysis from Parts 1 and 2 to assign a final score based on these definitions:

*   **5 Stars (Outstanding):** The review is defined by **Exceptional Enthusiasm**. The product exceeded expectations. It contains no significant criticisms. A review that concludes a product is an excellent value for its specific use case, even after comparing it to superior alternatives, also qualifies.
*   **4 Stars (Very Good):** The review's dominant theme is **Solid Satisfaction**. The product is good and met expectations. This is the correct rating for a review showing **Exceptional Enthusiasm** but which also includes a **Minor Issue**.
*   **3 Stars (Average/Mixed):** The review presents a balanced mix of notable pros and cons. Alternatively, the user's feeling is lukewarm ("it was just okay"). This can also apply to niche products where the recommendation is heavily qualified ("Good for fans of this specific genre, but not for everyone").
*   **2 Stars (Subpar):** The experience is primarily negative, driven by a **Significant Shortcoming**, even if the user mentions a minor positive aspect.
*   **1 Star (Defective/Unacceptable):** The review points to a **Critical Failure**. The user is angry or feels cheated, and the product failed at its most basic level.

Use this framework to analyze the provided review and assign the most fitting star rating.
