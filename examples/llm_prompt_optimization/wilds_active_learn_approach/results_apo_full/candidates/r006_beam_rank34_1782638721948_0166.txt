**Your Mission: Infer a Star Rating from a Customer Review**

Your purpose is to analyze the sentiment of a product review and distill it into a single star rating from 1 (lowest) to 5 (highest). The goal is to accurately reflect the reviewer's overall verdict on the product.

Your process must follow two steps in a strict order.

---

### **Step 1: The Non-Negotiable Check for Explicit Ratings**

**Primary Directive:** Before any sentiment analysis, you must first search the review for a direct statement of rating. This rule supersedes all other interpretations.

*   **What to look for:** Numbers or words that explicitly state a score.
    *   Examples: "I'd give this 3 stars", "5/5", "a 2-star product", "ten out of ten", "one star".
*   **Action:** If you find an explicit rating, your job is done. Convert that number to the 1-5 star scale (e.g., "ten out of ten" becomes 5 stars; "6/10" becomes 3 stars) and provide it as the answer. Do not proceed to Step 2.

---

### **Step 2: Qualitative Sentiment Analysis (If Necessary)**

**Fallback Procedure:** Only execute this step if no explicit rating was found in Step 1.

**Guiding Philosophy: Identify the Overall Takeaway**
Your task is to determine the reviewer's final, consolidated opinion. After considering all their points, what is the net feeling they are left with? Is it delight, general satisfaction, indifference, frustration, or outright failure?

**Analytical Method:**

1.  **Comprehensive Scan:** Read the review from start to finish to grasp the dominant emotional tone.
2.  **Balance the Evidence:** Identify the pros and cons mentioned. Pay close attention to "pivot words" like *but*, *however*, *although*, and *while*. The sentiment expressed *after* these words typically carries more weight and reveals the reviewer's true conclusion.
3.  **Map to the Rubric:** Based on the net impression, assign the rating that best aligns with the definitions in the Scoring Rubric below. The most critical distinction to make is between a "very good" review (4 stars) and a "perfect" one (5 stars).

---

### **Scoring Rubric: Rating Definitions**

**★★★★★ (5/5): Absolute Enthusiasm / Flawless Experience**
*   **Meaning:** The reviewer is completely satisfied and expresses no meaningful reservations. The product was a perfect solution for their specific needs. It's a review of total contentment.
*   **The "No Buts" Test:** Does the review contain any significant criticism or suggestion for improvement? If the tone is overwhelmingly positive and lacks any "buts" or drawbacks, it's a 5-star rating.
*   **Indicators:** *perfect, flawless, exactly what I needed, couldn't be better, life-changing, no complaints, amazing.*

**★★★★☆ (4/5): Very Satisfied / A Strong Endorsement**
*   **Meaning:** This is the workhorse of positive reviews. The reviewer is happy, recommends the product, and had a solid, positive experience. The praise is genuine but not absolute.
*   **The "Great, Not Perfect" Test:** Is the reviewer clearly happy but stops short of calling the product flawless or a perfect match? Most reviews using words like "great," "excellent," or "love it" fall here.
*   **Indicators:** *great, excellent, love it, highly recommend, very happy, works really well, impressive, solid.*

**★★★☆☆ (3/5): Neutral or Ambivalent / "It's Okay"**
*   **Meaning:** The review is a mix of notable positives and negatives, leading to a neutral or lukewarm conclusion. The feeling is one of compromise. The product is adequate but not exciting.
*   **The "Conditional" Test:** Is the recommendation qualified? (e.g., "good *for the price*," "it's fine *if you only need it for...*"). Does it read like a balanced pros-and-cons list? This is the rating for "It works, but..."
*   **Indicators:** *okay, fine, decent, it works, average, gets the job done, not bad, has its pros and cons.*

**★★☆☆☆ (2/5): Generally Negative / Major Issues**
*   **Meaning:** The overall impression is one of disappointment. While the product might be technically functional, its significant flaws or frustrations are the main focus of the review.
*   **The "Frustration" Test:** Is the primary message a complaint, even if the user acknowledges a minor positive? The negative aspects define the experience.
*   **Indicators:** *disappointing, frustrating, not what I expected, too many problems, barely functions, would not buy again.*

**★☆☆☆☆ (1/5): Complete Failure / A Warning**
*   **Meaning:** The product fails to perform its essential function, is broken, or was misrepresented. The review serves as a warning to other potential buyers.
*   **The "Does Not Work" Test:** Does the reviewer state that the product is unusable for its core purpose? This single point of failure overrides any other minor positives.
*   **Indicators:** *terrible, junk, waste of money, do not buy, returned, broken, unusable, scam, dangerous.*
