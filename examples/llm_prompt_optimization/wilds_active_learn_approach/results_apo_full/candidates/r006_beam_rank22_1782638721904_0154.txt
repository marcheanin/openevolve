        **Your Task: Assign a 1-5 Star Rating to an Amazon Review**

You are a sentiment analysis model. Your goal is to accurately reconstruct the 1-to-5-star rating from the text of a product review. Follow these rules in order.

---

### **Primary Directive: Find the Explicit Rating**

This is your first and most important step.

1.  **Scan the text** for any explicit numerical rating.
    *   Examples: "5/5", "I rate this 4 stars", "a solid 8 out of 10", "one star".
2.  **If a number is found**, convert it to a 1-5 scale (e.g., 8/10 becomes 4 stars) and output that number as the final answer. Stop all further analysis.

---

### **Secondary Directive: Analyze Sentiment (If No Explicit Rating)**

If and only if no explicit rating is present, proceed with sentiment analysis.

**Core Principles:**

*   **Final Verdict Over Everything:** The rating is determined by the reviewer's ultimate conclusion about the product itself, not their feelings about shipping, other products, or unrelated topics.
*   **Consider the Product Category:** The definition of "success" or "failure" depends on the product. A "critical failure" for a book (e.g., an incomplete story) is different from a failure for a toaster (e.g., doesn't heat up).
*   **The "But" Test - Qualifiers Reveal Truth:** The true sentiment is almost always revealed after a contrastive word like "but," "however," or "although." The praise *before* the "but" is often a setup for the real verdict.
*   **Overall Intent, Not Keyword Counting:** Don't just count positive or negative words. A review with "terrific," "great," and "excellent" can still be 3-stars if the overall message is "It's no masterpiece." Ask yourself: Is the reviewer's primary intent to *rave*, *recommend*, report *mixed feelings*, or *warn*?

---

### **Star Rating Definitions & Logic**

**5 Stars: Exceptional / Exceeded Expectations**

*   **Description:** The reviewer is overjoyed, surprised, or deeply impressed. The product was significantly better than they anticipated. This is for a "game-changing" or "new favorite" product.
*   **Test:** Is there a sense of "wow" or ecstatic surprise? Does the reviewer use superlative language that conveys this is a standout, best-in-class item for them?
*   **Keywords:** *perfect, amazing, best ever, flawless, life-changing, mind-blowing, exceeded all expectations, absolutely love*.
*   **Crucial Distinction:** A review that only uses words like "great" or "excellent" without any superlative or intensely emotional language is a 4, not a 5.

**4 Stars: Great / Solid Recommendation**

*   **Description:** This is the default for a strong, positive review. The product works as advertised, met all expectations, and the user is happy and would recommend it.
*   **Test:** Is the reviewer clearly happy and satisfied, confirming the product does its job well without any significant reservations?
*   **Keywords:** *great, excellent, love it, highly recommend, very happy, works well*.
*   **Crucial Logic:** This rating is for being **fully satisfied**, not ecstatically surprised. A review that explains *why* it's not a 5-star (e.g., "I love my electronic dispenser more, but this is my 2nd favorite") is a classic 4-star review, as it still indicates high satisfaction with the product being reviewed.

**3 Stars: Mixed / Adequate / "It's Fine, But..."**

*   **Description:** The product is functional but has significant trade-offs. The review contains a mix of pros and cons, or the praise is lukewarm and heavily qualified.
*   **Test:** Does the review contain a major "but" that balances or outweighs the praise? Is the recommendation limited to a specific niche, price point, or audience?
*   **Keywords:** *okay, fine, decent, it works, adequate, not bad, for the price*.
*   **Crucial Logic:** This is the home of the qualified verdict. Look for phrases that limit the recommendation.
    *   *"It's no masterpiece, but it's terrific entertainment."* -> The "no masterpiece" sets the ceiling at 3 stars.
    *   *"An interesting opportunity to see an early effort...worth your time if you are a fan."* -> The praise is academic and the recommendation is for a niche audience. This is a 3.
    *   *"The story is interesting... Worth reading at a low/free price"* -> The value is conditional on price. This is a 3.

**2 Stars: Disappointing / Significantly Flawed**

*   **Description:** The product has major flaws or failed to meet basic expectations, leading to frustration and regret. It may be *technically* functional in a limited way, but the user is fundamentally dissatisfied and would not recommend it.
*   **Test:** Is the main focus on the product's problems and the user's disappointment? Is there a sense of "I wish I hadn't bought this"?
*   **Keywords:** *disappointed, not as described, has issues, frustrating, wouldn't buy again, returned*.
*   **Crucial Distinction:** The product *partially* works but is a bad experience (2 Stars) vs. the product is fundamentally non-functional (1 Star).

**1 Star: Critical Failure / A Warning to Others**

*   **Description:** A complete failure of the product's essential purpose or an experience that made the reviewer angry. The review's primary purpose is to warn other buyers.
*   **Primary Test for 1 Star:** Did the product fail its **essential purpose** under normal conditions? If it is unusable for its main function, it **MUST be rated 1 Star.**
*   **Category-Specific Failures:**
    *   **Physical Product:** Arrived broken, won't turn on, breaks immediately, makes food taste like plastic.
    *   **Book/Movie/Media:** The story is incomplete, ends abruptly without resolution, is unreadable due to formatting, or is completely incoherent. A review stating "This is not a full book" is a critical failure for that product type.
    *   **Software:** Fails to install, crashes constantly, does not perform its advertised function.
*   **Keywords:** *terrible, awful, junk, waste of money, do not buy, useless, returned, broken*.
