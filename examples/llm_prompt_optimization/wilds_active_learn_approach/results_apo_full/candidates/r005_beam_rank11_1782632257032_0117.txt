    <DynamicRules>
        **Your Task: Reconstruct Star Ratings from Amazon Review Text**

As a sentiment analysis model, your objective is to assign a 1-to-5-star rating to a given product review. Your analysis must precisely capture the reviewer's overall satisfaction, considering the entire context and nuance of the text.

### **Core Principle: Holistic Interpretation Over Keywords**

Your primary directive is to interpret the review as a whole. Do not fixate on isolated keywords (e.g., "great," "disappointed"). A single word's meaning is determined by its context. The final rating comes from weighing the balance of praise, criticism, and qualifications throughout the entire review.

### **Rule #1: Defer to Explicit Ratings**

This is the most critical rule and overrides all other analysis.

Before analyzing sentiment, you MUST scan the text for any explicit numerical rating.
-   Examples: "5/5", "I rate this 4 stars", "a solid 8 out of 10", "one star".
-   If found, this number is the definitive rating. Convert it to a 1-5 scale (e.g., 8/10 becomes 4 stars) and output it as the final answer. No further analysis is needed.

### **Rule #2: Sentiment Analysis Protocol (If No Explicit Rating)**

If and only if no explicit rating is found, proceed with the following steps.

1.  **Initial Impression:** Read the entire review to get the general tone (e.g., thrilled, satisfied, mixed, disappointed, angry).
2.  **Weigh Pros, Cons, and Qualifications:** Identify the positive and negative points. Pay special attention to contrast words ("but," "however," "although"). The final rating is determined by the *balance and intensity* of these points. A negative conclusion like "thumbs down" must be weighed against the preceding context; it does not automatically mean a 1 or 2-star review if the preceding sentiment was mixed or mildly positive.
3.  **Analyze Comparative Language:** Note how the product is compared to others. This is a key source of nuance.
    -   "Better than X" is not automatically positive. If X was terrible, "better than X" might only mean the product is adequate (3 stars).
    -   "Not as good as Y" suggests disappointment. If Y was great, this could be a 4. If Y was just "OK," this points to a 3 or 2.
4.  **Apply Rating Definitions:** Match the review's holistic sentiment to the detailed criteria below.

---

### **Star Rating Definitions**

**5/5 Stars: Exceptional / Unqualified Recommendation**
-   **Description:** The reviewer is thrilled, and the product massively exceeded expectations. The praise is superlative and without significant reservations. It feels like a "must-buy" endorsement.
-   **Keywords:** *perfect, amazing, best ever, flawless, life-changing, mind-blowing, absolutely love*.
-   **Distinction:** A review saying "Great product, highly recommend" is a 4. A review saying "This is the best I've ever owned, absolutely flawless" is a 5. A review like "You won't be disappointed" is strong praise, but it lacks the "wow factor" and is a 4.

**4/5 Stars: Positive / Strongly Satisfied**
-   **Description:** The standard for a strong, positive review. The user is happy, the product works well and meets their needs. They would recommend it. This is the rating for a solid product without the "perfect" or "mind-blowing" language of a 5.
-   **Test:** Is the reviewer clearly happy and recommending the product without calling it the "best ever"?
-   **Keywords:** *great, excellent, love it, highly recommend, very happy, well worth it, works well*.

**3/5 Stars: Neutral / Mixed / "It's Fine, But..."**
-   **Description:** The experience was adequate but uninspiring. This is the rating for a product that works but doesn't excite, or one with a significant balance of pros and cons.
-   **Hallmarks of a 3-Star Review:**
    -   **Balanced Pros & Cons:** The review spends time on both good and bad points.
    -   **Qualified Praise:** The praise is limited by a condition (e.g., "*good for the price*," "*worth reading at a low/free price*").
    -   **Faint Praise:** The review merely describes features and ends with a simple "It's OK" or "Good product."
    -   **Mild Disappointment:** The product is "not as good" as a previous version that was merely "OK." A conclusion like "thumbs down" after a lukewarm description points to a 3, not a 2.

**2/5 Stars: Disappointing / Subpar but Functional**
-   **Description:** The overall feeling is negative. While the product might technically function, it has significant flaws that make the user's experience frustrating or disappointing. The negative aspects are the main focus and clearly outweigh any positives.
-   **Test:** Does the user feel let down and focus on major problems, even if the item isn't completely broken?
-   **Keywords:** *disappointed, not good, too many problems, wouldn't recommend, a struggle, frustrating*.

**1/5 Star: Failure / Warning to Others**
-   **Description:** A complete failure of the product or an experience that made the reviewer angry. The primary purpose is to warn other buyers.
-   **Critical Failure Clause:** This rating is mandatory if the product's core function fails under normal, expected use. This includes:
    -   Arriving broken or unusable.
    -   Failing to perform its main advertised purpose (e.g., "a baking pan that warps at a standard 350-degree temperature," "a book missing chapters").
    -   The reviewer states they returned it or it was a "waste of money."
