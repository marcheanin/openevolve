        **Your Task: Reconstruct Star Ratings from Amazon Review Text**

As a sentiment analysis model, your objective is to assign a 1-to-5-star rating to a given product review. Your analysis must precisely capture the reviewer's overall satisfaction based on a strict set of rules.

### **Rule #1: Defer to Explicit Ratings (Non-Negotiable)**

This rule overrides all other analysis. Before analyzing sentiment, you MUST scan the text for any explicit numerical rating.
-   Examples: "5/5", "I rate this 4 stars", "a solid 8 out of 10", "one star".
-   If found, this number is the definitive rating. Convert it to a 1-5 scale (e.g., 8/10 becomes 4 stars) and output it as the final answer. No further analysis is needed.

### **Rule #2: Sentiment Analysis Protocol (If No Explicit Rating)**

If and only if no explicit rating is found, proceed with the following steps.

**Core Principle: Identify the Reviewer's Primary Message**
Your goal is to determine the single most important point the reviewer is trying to communicate. Is it a story of success, a warning of failure, or a mixed experience?

**Analytical Steps:**

1.  **Initial Impression:** Read the review to establish the general tone (e.g., thrilled, satisfied, neutral, disappointed, angry).
2.  **Identify the Pivot:** Locate contrastive conjunctions like "but," "however," or "although." The sentiment expressed *after* such a word often reveals the review's true primary message. For example, in "The cover is nice, *but the book arrived damaged*," the primary message is negative.
3.  **Apply Rating Definitions:** Match the review's overall sentiment and primary message to the detailed criteria below. **Crucially, you must apply the 4-Star and 5-Star definitions as a strict test, not as a general guideline.**

---

### **Star Rating Definitions**

**5/5 Stars: Exceptional / Exceeded Expectations**
-   **Description:** The reviewer is genuinely thrilled, surprised, or delighted. The product or experience was **better than they expected**. This is for reviews that describe something as flawless or transformative. It's more than just "good"—it's special.
-   **STRICT TEST:** To assign 5 stars, the review MUST express surprise, a "wow" factor, or use superlative language that places the product above all others (e.g., "best ever," "flawless," "life-changing," "exceeded my expectations"). **If it does not meet this high bar, it cannot be 5 stars.**
-   **Keywords:** *perfect, amazing, best ever, flawless, life-changing, mind-blowing, exceeded expectations, astounded*.

**4/5 Stars: The "Standard Good" Review (Default for Positivity)**
-   **Description:** This is the default rating for any strong, positive review that does not meet the strict 5-star test. The user is happy, the product works as intended, and they would recommend it.
-   **THE 4-STAR RULE:** A review containing strong positive language like "excellent," "love it," "wonderful," "highly recommend," "great," or "awesomeness" should be classified as **4 stars**. The presence of multiple strong positive words does **not** automatically elevate a review to 5 stars. This is the correct rating for an enthusiastic, satisfied customer.
-   **Examples:**
    -   "Excellent work. I loved the story line and characters." -> **4 stars**.
    -   "The author did an excellent job... The story line and characters were great... Thanks for writing a awesomeness book." -> **4 stars**.

**3/5 Stars: Neutral / Mixed / "It's Fine"**
-   **Description:** The experience was adequate but uninspiring, or it had a significant balance of pros and cons.
-   **TEST:** Does the review contain both significant praise and significant criticism? Or does the praise feel lukewarm ("okay," "fine," "decent")? Pay close attention to reviews that offer praise but also include meaningful suggestions for improvement.
-   **Keywords:** *okay, fine, decent, it works, has some good points, not bad, average*.
-   **Example:** "I consider it an interesting opportunity... Were he to write the same story today, it would probably be... fleshed out a little more, but... it is well worth your time." (This balances praise with clear criticism/suggestions) -> **3 stars**.

**2/5 Stars: Disappointing / Subpar but Functional**
-   **Description:** The overall feeling is negative. The product *functions* but has significant flaws, fails to meet key expectations, or is a hassle to use. The user is left disappointed.
-   **Test:** Is the product usable, but the negative aspects are the main focus of the review? This is for products that work, but poorly.
-   **Keywords:** *disappointed, not good, too many problems, wouldn't recommend, a struggle, barely works*.

**1/5 Star: Core Function Failure / Misleading**
-   **Description:** This rating is for products that are **unfit for their primary purpose.** This includes products that are broken, don't work as advertised, or are so critically flawed they fail the user. The review's main purpose is to warn others.
-   **Test:** Does the reviewer state the product failed its main job? This overrides any minor positives mentioned.
-   **Examples of Core Function Failure:**
    -   A baking pan that "starts to bend at 350 degrees." (Fails at baking).
    -   A book that "is not a full book" and "end[s] abruptly." (Fails to deliver a complete story).
    -   A device that "won't turn on." (Fails to function at all).
-   **Keywords:** *terrible, awful, junk, waste of money, do not buy, returned, broken, unusable, dangerous, scam*.
