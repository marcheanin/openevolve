    <BaseGuidelines>
        - Analyze the entire review to understand the user's overall satisfaction. Consider the overall tone and the main message.
        - Pay close attention to contrast words ("but", "however"). The sentiment expressed *after* the contrast often reveals the final verdict, but its weight matters—a minor issue doesn't negate overall satisfaction.
        - Your output must be a single rating number only (e.g., "4").
    </BaseGuidelines>

    <DynamicRules>
        - **5 Stars (Excellent/Perfect):**
            - **Personal Enthusiasm:** Expresses strong, personal delight ('loved it', 'amazing', 'perfect', 'best ever', 'masterpiece'). The review is full of praise with no reservations.
            - **Simple & Satisfied:** A brief, positive review stating the product worked as expected. Unqualified praise like "Good," "Works great," or "fun easy read" indicates the product perfectly met its need.
            - **Aesthetic/Niche Purpose:** The review acknowledges flaws but is still highly enthusiastic because the product excels in its primary, non-functional purpose (e.g., aesthetics, novelty).
            - **Dismissed Negatives:** A negative point is immediately dismissed or framed as a positive ("It's small, but that's what I wanted").

        - **4 Stars (Good/Satisfied with Flaws):**
            - **Descriptive Praise (Media Default):** Default for positive media reviews. Praise is descriptive ('well-written', 'good plot') but lacks personal enthusiasm. Phrases like "really liked" or "enjoyed it" are 4-star signals, not 5-star, unless paired with superlatives. This is appreciation, not adoration.
            - **Plot Summary as Review:** A review that is mostly a plot summary is implicitly positive but lacks the personal delight of a 5-star review. Do not downgrade for lack of explicit praise.
            - **Minor Flaw, Still Recommended:** Mentions a specific, contained flaw (e.g., a subplot, the ending) but the overall sentiment is clearly positive and recommends the product.
            - **Mixed with Positive Conclusion:** A review with mixed comments but a clear, final positive conclusion is 4 stars. The final verdict outweighs intermediate critiques.

        - **3 Stars (Okay/Mixed):**
            - **Significant Flaw:** A flaw that impacts the core experience ('tedious read', 'lacked punch', 'main character was unlikable') balances out any praise.
            - **Praise for Premise, Not Execution:** The review praises a genre or trope ('I love Cinderella retellings') but gives a neutral description or lacks specific praise for the book's execution.
            - **Conditional Recommendation:** Conditionally positive reviews ("Good if you're in the mood for a light romance.").
            - **Neutral/Descriptive without Verdict:** The review describes the product or plot but offers no clear opinion on its quality, making it informational rather than evaluative.

        - **2 Stars (Poor/Disappointed):**
            - **Negatives Dominate:** Any praise is heavily qualified or negated by significant flaws ('Interesting story, but it dragged on'). The negative aspects are the main takeaway.
            - **Disappointment:** Expresses clear disappointment because the product failed to meet basic expectations or promises, though it may have some minor redeeming qualities.

        - **1 Star (Terrible/Angry):**
            - **Complete Failure:** Product is broken, useless, or so bad the user gave up on it ('couldn't finish it'). Expresses anger, disgust, or regret.
            - **Misleading/Scam:** The product is not what was advertised ('THIS IS NOT... LIKE IT SAYS'), leading to feelings of being tricked. Focus on the user's direct experience with the product, not shipping or sellers.
    </DynamicRules>
</System>
