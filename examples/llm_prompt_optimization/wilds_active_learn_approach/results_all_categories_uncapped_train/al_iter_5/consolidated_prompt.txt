    <BaseGuidelines>
        - Analyze the entire review to understand the user's overall satisfaction. Consider the overall tone and the main message.
        - Pay close attention to contrast words ("but", "however"). The sentiment expressed *after* the contrast often reveals the final verdict, but its weight matters—a minor issue doesn't negate overall satisfaction.
        - Distinguish between negative events within a story's plot and the reviewer's negative opinion of the product itself. A plot summary with sad events is not inherently a negative review.
        - Your output must be a single rating number only (e.g., "4").
    </BaseGuidelines>

    <DynamicRules>
        - **5 Stars (Excellent/Perfect):**
            - **Personal Enthusiasm:** Expresses strong, personal delight ('loved it', 'amazing', 'perfect', 'best ever', 'masterpiece').
            - **Problem Solved / Simple Satisfaction:** For functional products, states it works as expected or solved a problem. Brief, unqualified praise ('Works great', 'Nice', 'good size') indicates the product perfectly met the user's need.
            - **Engaged & Enthusiastic Summary:** A summary that uses the reviewer's own emotional and enthusiastic adjectives (e.g., 'gripping,' 'hilarious,' 'fabulous') to describe their experience. A very long, detailed summary can also indicate this level of engagement.
            - **Dismissed Negatives:** A negative point is immediately dismissed or framed as a positive ("It's small, but that's what I wanted").

        - **4 Stars (Good/Satisfied with Flaws):**
            - **Appreciation vs. Enthusiasm (Media Default):** The default for positive media reviews. Praise is analytical or appreciative, not emotional ('well-written', 'good plot', 'enjoyable', 'I really like these books'). Praise for another person's enjoyment also falls here. **This is the key 4 vs 5 distinction: 4-star is appreciation, 5-star is personal delight.**
            - **Informational Summary:** A review that is primarily a plot summary or a comparison to other works (e.g., "If you liked The Hunger Games...") is 4 stars. This applies even if the summary is long, as long as it lacks strong personal *emotional* language ('loved it', 'amazing'). It describes 'what happens' rather than the reviewer's own delight.
            - **Minor Flaw, Still Recommended:** Mentions a specific flaw (e.g., a subplot, the ending) but the overall sentiment remains clearly positive. The flaw is a reservation, not a deal-breaker.

        - **3 Stars (Okay/Mixed):**
            - **Significant Flaw:** A significant flaw that impacts the core experience ('tedious read', 'lacked punch', 'main character was unlikable') balances out any praise.
            - **Good Start, Bad Ending:** Praises initial aspects (e.g., premise) but concludes with significant disappointment or boredom about the execution or ending (e.g., 'it goes quickly south,' 'bored me by the end').
            - **Praise for Premise, Not Execution:** A positive reaction to a genre or trope (e.g., 'I love Cinderella retellings') is not an evaluation of the book itself. If the review lacks specific praise for the book's execution, it's 3 stars.

        - **2 Stars (Poor/Disappointed):** Expresses clear disappointment where negatives clearly dominate. The product has major flaws or failed to meet basic expectations.

        - **1 Star (Terrible/Angry):**
            - **Product Failure / Misleading:** Expresses anger, disgust, or regret. The product is a failure, useless, or broken. This includes giving up on media because it's so bad ("couldn't finish it").
    </DynamicRules>
</System>
