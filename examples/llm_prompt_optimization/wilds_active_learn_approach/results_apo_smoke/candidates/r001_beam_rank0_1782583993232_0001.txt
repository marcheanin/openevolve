**Rating Analysis Workflow**

1.  **Assess the Dominant Sentiment:** First, determine if the overall tone is positive, negative, or a mix of both.
2.  **Gauge the Sentiment Intensity:** Evaluate how strong the emotion is (e.g., weak, moderate, or strong).
3.  **Analyze for Contrast:** Look for pivotal words like "but," "although," or "however." The opinion expressed *after* such a contrast typically defines the final sentiment.
4.  **Determine the User Outcome:** Does the user express delight, satisfaction, ambivalence, disappointment, or regret?
5.  **Assign the Final Star Rating:** Convert the complete analysis into a 1-to-5 star score using the guidelines below.

---

**Rating Scale Breakdown**

*   **5 Stars (Exceptional):** Represents overwhelming satisfaction and strong advocacy. The user is delighted and highly recommends the product/service.
    *   *Indicators:* "perfect," "best ever," "must-buy," "life-changing," "amazing"

*   **4 Stars (Good):** Indicates a positive experience with only minor reservations or suggestions for improvement. The user is satisfied and would likely recommend it.
    *   *Indicators:* "very good," "impressed," "mostly happy," "works well," "enjoyed"

*   **3 Stars (Average/Neutral):** Signifies a mixed or neutral opinion. This could be due to balanced pros and cons, an undecided user, or a purely factual description of an average experience.
    *   *Indicators:* "it's fine," "average," "has pros and cons," "not bad," "okay"

*   **2 Stars (Subpar):** Reflects clear dissatisfaction, even if some minor positive aspects are mentioned. The product did not meet key expectations.
    *   *Indicators:* "disappointing," "doesn't work well," "not what I expected," "major flaws"

*   **1 Star (Poor):** Denotes extreme disappointment, frustration, or a complete failure of the product/service. The user expresses regret and may warn others.
    *   *Indicators:* "awful," "useless," "avoid at all costs," "regret buying," "terrible"

---

**Key Interpretation Principles**

*   **Emphasis on the Conclusion:** The final sentences or overall summary of a review carry the most weight in determining the final score.
*   **The Power of Contrast:** The sentiment following a contrast word ("but," "however") overrides the sentiment that precedes it.
*   **Intensity Trumps Position:** A single, powerful emotional phrase (e.g., "complete disaster") can outweigh several milder, contradictory statements.
*   **Repetition as an Amplifier:** Repeating a sentiment (e.g., "good price, good quality") reinforces its strength and polarity.
*   **The Role of Negation:** Pay close attention to negations ("not," "didn't") which reverse the meaning of the words that follow (e.g., "not bad" is weakly positive or neutral).

---

**User Outcome Rules of Thumb**

*   **Strong Recommendation/Enthusiasm:** Points to a 5-star rating.
*   **General Satisfaction/Willingness to Reuse:** Suggests a 4-star rating.
*   **Ambivalence/Uncertainty:** Typically results in a 3-star rating.
*   **Regret/Warning Others Not to Buy:** Strongly indicates a 1 or 2-star rating.

---

**Handling Special Cases**

*   **Sarcasm:** Identify when positive language is used ironically to convey a negative meaning (e.g., "Great. It broke in a day.").
*   **Critical Failure:** If a product fails to perform its core advertised function, assign a 1-star rating, regardless of any minor positives.
*   **Purely Factual Reviews:** If a review is descriptive and lacks any emotional or opinionated language, default to a neutral 3-star rating.
*   **Forced Engagement:** Statements like "I struggled to get through it" or "I forced myself to finish" imply a negative experience, warranting a rating of 2 stars or lower.
